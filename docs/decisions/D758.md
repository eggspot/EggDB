## D758 The snapping mechanism and l-diversity — Decided by the code-only session (2026-11-12, D355); roadmap M6b, differential privacy: the snapping mechanism and l-diversity (D52, D245)

- `snapping_mechanism(value, sensitivity, epsilon, bound)` follows Mironov (2012, "On significance of the least
  significant bits for differential privacy"): with λ = sensitivity / epsilon and Λ the smallest power of two at
  least λ, it returns clamp_B(round_Λ(clamp_B(value) + S·λ·ln U)), S a random sign and U uniform over every double
  in (0, 1) (a geometric exponent from random bits, then 52 random mantissa bits), so U reaches the small values a
  53-bit fraction cannot. Rounding to a multiple of a power of two is exact in binary floating point. `bound` must
  exceed λ (22023 otherwise). It relies on the platform's `ln`, which is close to but not always correctly rounded,
  a known gap from the paper's assumption.
- `dp_count` and `dp_sum` take an optional last `bound` and then answer through the snapping mechanism instead of
  `laplace_noise`; without it they behave as before (D748), and `dp_avg` keeps using them unchanged.
- `eggdb_privacy.l_diversity(schema, table, quasi_identifiers, sensitive_column)` returns distinct l-diversity: the
  smallest count of distinct sensitive values within any group of rows that share the quasi-identifiers, beside
  `k_anonymity`. `entropy_l_diversity` returns entropy l-diversity, the smallest exp(entropy) of the sensitive values
  in a group; NULL sensitive values are left out of both measures.
- Recursive (c, l)-diversity and t-closeness are left.
