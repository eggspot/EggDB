## D1114 Datagrams in a completion queue of their own, with batched receives — Decided by the code-only session (2026-11-13, D355); roadmap M1 (datagrams in the network completion queue for QUIC, D315)

- **A `DatagramQueue` beside `NetQueue`, not new `NetOp` variants.** `NetOp`, `NetCompletion` and every backend are
  typed by a listener and a stream; a datagram variant would add a third socket type to all of them (86 match sites
  over six backends) for operations QUIC runs on one UDP socket from one thread. `eggdb_platform::DatagramQueue<D>`
  takes any `DatagramSocket`, the simulation's included, and keeps `NetQueue`'s shape: `submit` with a tag, `reap`,
  `reap_timeout`, `cancel`, `in_flight`, `capacity`, `QueueFull` hand-backs and the same capacity limits
  (`NetQueueOptions`).
- **Receives complete in submission order.** Each takes the next datagram; a reap first takes what is already
  waiting without blocking, then waits in slices of at most 250 ms, counting the waits it asked for, so a timed reap
  runs on the simulated clock in a simulation.
- **Sends go out at submission** and complete at the next reap: a UDP send never waits for the peer, so there is
  nothing to queue.
- **Batched receives in the socket trait.** `DatagramSocket::try_receive_from` (no wait) and `receive_batch`
  (wait for the first, then take every waiting one, up to one per buffer) have defaults built on `receive_from`;
  `OsDatagramSocket` overrides them with `recvfrom(MSG_DONTWAIT)` on Linux, Android and Apple's systems and one
  `recvmmsg` call for the rest of a batch on Linux and Android, reading the sender from the address storage
  (Apple's `sa_len` byte first, Linux's 16-bit family). Other systems use the defaults.
- **Left:** io_uring `RECVMSG` and IOCP `WSARecvFrom` completions in the native rings, once a QUIC benchmark shows
  the batched receive is the cost, and generic segmentation offload (`UDP_SEGMENT`) for sends.

**Status:** code written, unbuilt (code-only mode, D355).
