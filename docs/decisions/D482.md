## D482 AMQP 0-9-1 headers exchanges, exclusive and auto-delete queues — Decided by the code-only session (2026-11-11, D355); roadmap M16, D214

- Field tables are decoded (`eggdb-amqp`'s `field_table`: booleans, every integer width, floats,
  decimals, strings and byte arrays, arrays, timestamps, nested tables and void; at most 1,024
  entries and 8 levels), and so are the `headers` of a message's content properties.
- `exchange.declare` takes type `headers`, and `amq.headers` and `amq.match` exist from the start.
  A binding keeps its arguments; a headers exchange routes a message to the bindings whose
  arguments match its headers under `x-match`: `all` (the default) or `any`, ignoring arguments
  that start with `x-`, or `all-with-x`/`any-with-x` comparing them too, as RabbitMQ does. A void
  argument asks only that the header exists; integers compare by value whatever their width.
  `queue.unbind` on a headers exchange removes the binding with the same arguments.
- `queue.declare` with `exclusive` ties the queue to the declaring connection: another connection
  that declares, binds, consumes, gets, purges or deletes it gets `RESOURCE_LOCKED` (405), and the
  queue is deleted with its bindings when the connection ends.
- `queue.declare` with `auto-delete` deletes the queue (and its bindings) when its last consumer
  goes away, by `basic.cancel`, a closed channel or a closed connection, once it has had one, as
  AMQP 0-9-1 describes.
- Exclusive and auto-delete state lives in the server's memory, as RabbitMQ's does for such
  transient queues; a restart forgets it, leaving such queues as ordinary queues. Durable
  exchanges and bindings across restarts are still not written.
