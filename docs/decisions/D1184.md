## D1184 Kerberos logins through GSSAPI, in our own clean room — Decided by the code-only session (2026-11-15, D355); roadmap M6b (D22, D185); security-critical (D35)

- **What users get.** A rule `alice gss keytab=/etc/eggdb/eggdb.keytab krb_realm=EXAMPLE.COM` (or `all gss ...`) lets a PostgreSQL client with a Kerberos ticket log in without a password: psql, libpq, JDBC with `gsslib=gssapi`, Npgsql with `Integrated Security`, and Windows clients through SSPI's SPNEGO. The principal `alice@EXAMPLE.COM` logs in as role `alice` (`include_realm=1` keeps the realm in the role name, as PostgreSQL's option does); the role must exist with `LOGIN`, and its privileges apply (D183).
- **Written from the specifications, in `eggdb_auth::kerberos`.** RFC 4120's AP-REQ, Ticket, EncTicketPart, Authenticator and AP-REP in DER over the BER reader the LDAP client already had; RFC 3961's n-fold and key derivation and RFC 3962's `aes256-cts-hmac-sha1-96` and `aes128-cts-hmac-sha1-96` (AES-CBC with ciphertext stealing, HMAC-SHA1-96, string-to-key over PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA1) over `eggdb-crypto`'s AES and HMAC; RFC 1964 and RFC 4121's GSS-API token framing with the 0x8003 checksum's flags; RFC 4178's SPNEGO `NegTokenInit`/`NegTokenResp`; and the MIT keytab format (`0x0502`). No Kerberos library is linked. Only the two AES types are accepted: a ticket under DES, RC4 or 3DES is refused, as current KDCs and RFC 8429 recommend.
- **Checks.** The service ticket must decrypt with a keytab key for its service principal, realm, encryption type and key version; its end time (and start time) must hold with five minutes' clock skew; the authenticator must decrypt with the ticket's session key and name the ticket's client; its time must be within five minutes, and its `(principal, ctime, cusec)` must not have been seen in the last ten minutes (an in-memory replay cache of at most 100 000 entries, refusing when full); the client's realm must be `krb_realm` when given; the user name the client sent must be the principal's role name. A failure is reported as a wrong password (`28P01`), logged with its reason, and throttled like SCRAM failures. When the client asks for mutual authentication (the GSS checksum's flag), the server answers with an AP-REP under the session key, which libpq waits for.
- **On the wire.** pgwire sends `AuthenticationGSS`, reads the client's `GSSResponse` (tokens up to 64 KiB, enough for Active Directory tickets with a PAC), sends `AuthenticationGSSContinue` with the AP-REP when asked, then `AuthenticationOk`. GSSAPI transport encryption (`gssencmode=require`) is not offered: clients fall back to TLS or plain connections, as against a PostgreSQL server built without it. MySQL and TDS listeners refuse `gss` rules with a reason (integrated Windows logins over TDS are left).
- **Proof.** Unit tests check n-fold and string-to-key against RFC 3961 and RFC 3962's vectors, sealing and opening at every length around the block size, the keytab reader, hostile tokens, and an AP-REQ built with known keys that is accepted once (with its AP-REP decrypted back), refused when replayed, expired or from another realm. CI's new `kerberos` job runs MIT Kerberos's KDC for a realm of its own, gets alice a ticket with `kinit`, and logs in with psql over GSSAPI.
- **Security review.** As a D35 change, it needs its own security-only review pass when the tree is built: key usages, constant-time MAC comparison, the replay cache, and that no path accepts a ticket without the authenticator's checks.
- Left: GSSAPI transport encryption, constrained delegation, PAC group mapping, TDS integrated logins, and `kvno`-less keytab rotation beyond the newest-first match.

**Status:** code written, unbuilt (code-only mode, D355).
