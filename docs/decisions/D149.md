## D149 The rest of Oracle's datetime and number functions: Oracle's intervals on the engine's `interval`, the full datetime and number format models, `TIME_ZONE` as the session's time zone, `LISTAGG` over the array aggregate, `FETCH ... PERCENT` and `WITH TIES` in the row window — Decided by the build loop (by judgement and Oracle's public SQL Language Reference, 2026-10-30); roadmap item 42b (batch part 3.05), applies D1, D9, D10, D19, D108, D113, D119, D126; supersedes in part D113 and D126

Item 42 (D126) left a list of Oracle behaviours for 42b. This decision records how they are
built. Nothing new is stored: no type tag, table-definition field or format version changes,
so there is no D35 review. The code lives in Oracle modules of `eggdb-sql`:
`oracle_datetime` (the datetime format model, extended), `oracle_date_functions` (date units,
`ROUND`/`TRUNC`, `MONTHS_BETWEEN`, `NEXT_DAY`, zones), `oracle_intervals`, `oracle_number_model`
(moved out of `oracle_functions` and extended), `oracle_numerals` (Roman numerals and English
number words, shared by `RM`, `RN`, `SP` and `YEAR`) and `oracle_listagg`.

**Intervals are the engine's `interval`.** `INTERVAL YEAR [(p)] TO MONTH` is `interval year to
month` and `INTERVAL DAY [(p)] TO SECOND [(s)]` is `interval day to second(s)`, with `s` above 6
kept as 6, as `TIMESTAMP(p)` is. Oracle allows no other field pair for a column (`ORA-00905`).
A year-to-month value holds only months, a day-to-second value only days and microseconds,
normalised so the microseconds stay within a day (`INTERVAL '25' HOUR` is one day and one hour).
The leading precision is checked for literals (`ORA-01873`, default 2) and accepted but not
kept for columns, since the type has no room for it and adding one would change the file
format; a literal whose last field is not `SECOND` has no fractional digits. Values print in
Oracle's form, `+01-02` and `+04 05:12:10.222000`, with at least two leading digits and the
type's fractional digits (six when unknown); Oracle shows the nine-digit precisions of its
arithmetic results, a known difference. Literals are constants built by the parser, text meets
an interval as `TO_YMINTERVAL`/`TO_DSINTERVAL` read it (Oracle's form or ISO 8601), and
`EXTRACT` takes the fields of the value's kind (`ORA-30076` otherwise).

**Arithmetic by Oracle's rules.** `TIMESTAMP - TIMESTAMP` is an `INTERVAL DAY TO SECOND` when
either operand's declared type is a `TIMESTAMP` with fractional digits or a time zone; two
`DATE`s (and operands of unknown type) keep the number of days. `datetime ± interval` with
months moves the calendar month and refuses a day the month lacks (`ORA-01839`, as the
Datetime/Interval Arithmetic section shows for `DATE '2024-01-31' + INTERVAL '1' MONTH`) where
PostgreSQL's arithmetic clamps; the year stays within 4713 BC to 9999 (`ORA-01841`).
`interval * n` and `interval / n` round to whole months or microseconds; mixing the two kinds is
`ORA-30081`. The Oracle hook runs before the engine's own interval arithmetic.

**The datetime format model** is a table of elements read longest first. New in reading models:
`J`, `DDD`, `D`, `DAY`, `DY`, `SSSSS`, `RM`, `SYYYY`, `Y,YYY`, AD/BC in both spellings and the
zone elements (only in `TO_TIMESTAMP_TZ`; `ORA-01821` in `TO_DATE` and `TO_TIMESTAMP`).
Writing adds `CC`, `SCC`, `YEAR`, `SYEAR`, `IYYY` to `I`, `IW`, `WW`, `W`, `Q` and the suffixes
`TH`, `SP`, `SPTH`/`THSP`, which reading refuses with `ORA-01820`. The checks follow Oracle's
error list: a field written twice is `ORA-01810` for the same element and the field's own error
for two spellings (`01812` year, `01813` hour, `01814`, `01815`, `01816` month, `01817` day of
week); `J` with `DDD` `01811`; `HH24` with a meridian `01818`; `SYYYY` with BC `01819`; a Julian
day disagreeing with the year, month or day `01831`/`01833`/`01834`; `SSSSS` disagreeing with
the hour, minute or second `01836` to `01838`; a day name disagreeing with the date `01835`. By
judgement, `DDD` with a month or day of month that disagrees is `ORA-01839` (Oracle documents
no error of its own for it). `DS`, `DL` and `TS` expand to the AMERICA territory's documented
equivalents, `MM/DD/RRRR` and `fmDay, Month dd, yyyy` (so `December 1`), and, by judgement,
`HH:MI:SS AM` for `TS`; they combine only with each other and blanks (`ORA-01821`). Years run
from 4713 BC to 9999 on the proleptic Gregorian calendar the engine counts with, where Oracle
uses the Julian calendar before 15 October 1582 (a known difference for old dates' `J` and day
names); 4714 BC is accepted so that every Julian day from 1 reads. A `TIMESTAMP WITH TIME ZONE`
keeps its instant only (D126), so `TZH`, `TZM` and `TZR` print the session's offset and `TZD`
nothing; `TZR` reads offsets and the `UTC`/`GMT` family, other regions are `ORA-01882` until
EggDB has a time zone database. `NLS_TIMESTAMP_TZ_FORMAT` defaults to Oracle's
`DD-MON-RR HH.MI.SSXFF AM TZR`.

**Dates.** `ROUND` and `TRUNC` take every unit of the "ROUND and TRUNC Date Functions" table:
the century from year xx01 (rounding up from xx51), the year (up on July 1), the ISO year (from
the Monday of its week 1, up from July 1), the quarter (up on the 16th day of its second month),
the month (up on the 16th), the weeks `WW`, `IW`, `W` and `DAY` (America's weeks start on
Sunday; up from three and a half days), the day (up at noon), the hour and the minute; an
unknown unit stays `ORA-01821`. `MONTHS_BETWEEN` is whole when the days of month agree or both
are last days, otherwise months plus the difference in days and seconds over 31-day months,
computed in decimal (fewer digits than Oracle's 38, the known division difference). `NEXT_DAY`
takes a name whose first three letters are an American day's abbreviation, ignoring the rest as
Oracle documents (`ORA-01846` otherwise), and keeps the time of day. `FROM_TZ`,
`SYS_EXTRACT_UTC` and `TZ_OFFSET` work on offsets.

**`TIME_ZONE` is the session's time zone.** `ALTER SESSION SET TIME_ZONE = '-08:00'` (also an
`UTC`-family name, `LOCAL` and `DBTIMEZONE`, both `+00:00` since the engine never reads the
operating system's zone) sets the session's `TimeZone` setting to the POSIX name the engine
already reads (`UTC+08:00`), so the statement clock, `TIMESTAMP WITH TIME ZONE` and `SYSDATE`
follow it, and `RESET`/`DISCARD` treat it as any setting. `SESSIONTIMEZONE` and `DBTIMEZONE`
are read when the statement is parsed, as `USER` is.

**The number format model** moves to `oracle_number_model` and gains `EEEE`, `RN`, `X`, `TM`,
`L`, `C`, `U`, `V`, `B`, `PR` and a trailing `S`, laid out as the Format Models page's
"Results of Number Conversions" table shows: `  1.2E+02` (two leading blanks), `L` padding its
symbol to twelve characters (`            $123.45`), `FM` keeping the zeros a `0` element asks
for. By judgement where the page is silent: `RN` right-aligns in fifteen characters and is all
`#` outside 1 to 3999; `X` has one leading blank unless `0` or `FM` is given and is all `#` for a
negative or too large value; the ISO currency is `USD` and the dual one `$`; `TO_NUMBER` reads
`$`, `L`, `C`, `U`, `PR`, a trailing `S`, `EEEE`, `X` and, beyond what the page lists, `RN`,
and refuses `V` (`ORA-01481`).

**`LISTAGG`** is the array aggregate over each value written as Oracle's text (NULLs kept, so
`DISTINCT` and `WITHIN GROUP (ORDER BY ...)` reuse the aggregate's own machinery), joined by an
Oracle function that leaves NULLs out and returns NULL for none. The limit is 32,767 bytes, the
`VARCHAR2` limit EggDB's Oracle columns already allow (Oracle's `MAX_STRING_SIZE = EXTENDED`):
past it `ON OVERFLOW ERROR` (the default) is `ORA-01489`, and `TRUNCATE` keeps whole values,
then the delimiter, the indicator (default `...`) and, unless `WITHOUT COUNT`, the number of
values left out in parentheses. `WITHIN GROUP` is optional, as in 23ai. `LISTAGG ... OVER`
waits for analytic aggregates.

**`FETCH ... PERCENT` and `WITH TIES`** stay in the query's limit expression, marked by internal
Oracle function names, and the row window reads them (`RowLimit` in `execute.rs`): a percent is
of the rows the query finds before `OFFSET`, rounded up, and needs every row, so a percent query
reads them all; `WITH TIES` keeps the rows after the last one that sort equal to it, so it always
takes the sorting path even when an index gives the order. Set operations apply the same window.

**Not done here, still listed in sql.md:** languages and territories other than American and
`NLS_CURRENCY`, offsets kept inside `TIMESTAMP WITH TIME ZONE`, `AT TIME ZONE`, `E`/`EE`,
`TO_DATE(... DEFAULT ... ON CONVERSION ERROR)`, `LISTAGG ... OVER`, division at 38 digits,
`NUMBER(p)` range checks by `p`, `VARCHAR2` byte semantics, dates by `NLS_DATE_FORMAT` in result
sets and the other D126 follow-ups not named above.

Supersedes in part D113 (Oracle's `INTERVAL YEAR TO MONTH` and `INTERVAL DAY TO SECOND` were
`0A000`) and D126 (`TIMESTAMP - TIMESTAMP` as days, `TIME_ZONE` as `ORA-03001`, `J`, `DDD`, `D`,
`DAY`, `DY` and `SSSSS` refused as input, years 1 to 9999, and the 42b list).
