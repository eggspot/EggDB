## D1225 Distances and lengths on the WGS 84 spheroid — Decided by the code-only session (2026-11-17, D355); roadmap M12, geography on the WGS 84 spheroid (D204)

- **What users get.** PostGIS's `ST_DistanceSpheroid(a, b [, spheroid])` and `ST_LengthSpheroid(geom [, spheroid])`: metres along the WGS 84 ellipsoid (or a spheroid given as PostGIS writes it, `SPHEROID["name",a,rf]`, an inverse flattening of 0 meaning a sphere) between two longitude/latitude points and along line strings, multi line strings and collections. They answer within a millimetre where `ST_DistanceSphere`'s mean-radius sphere is off by up to about 0.5%.
- **How.** A new `geodesic` module in `eggdb-geo` with Vincenty's inverse formula, written from his 1975 paper's published equations: iterate the longitude on the auxiliary sphere to 1e-12 radians (at most 200 rounds), then the series for the distance. Coincident points are 0.
- **Decided by research.** Vincenty's method does not converge for nearly antipodal points; EggDB answers those with an error (`22023`) rather than a silently rough figure. Karney's method, which always converges, is the better long-term answer and is recorded as left.
- **Left.** Karney's geodesics for nearly antipodal points, the `geography` type measuring on the spheroid by default (`ST_Distance` and `ST_Length` of geographies), areas on the spheroid, and Z and M coordinates.
- **Proof.** `eggdb-geo` tests: Vincenty's own Flinders Peak to Buninyong example (54,972.271 m), one degree on the equator (111,319.491 m), a nearly antipodal pair refused, spheroid text parsed and refused, a line's length; `geo_tests.rs` runs both SQL functions.

**Status:** code written, unbuilt (code-only mode, D355).
