## D1087 Write-amplification and flash-wear budgets, and battery-aware checkpoints — Decided by the code-only session (2026-11-13, D355); roadmap M17 (D41, D1083, D1085)

- **Counted where the flash is.** `WearLevelled` keeps `WearStats`: blocks written by the caller, data sectors programmed (wear-levelling moves included), journal bytes (records, snapshots, headers) and erases (journal slots included). `write_amplification_milli` is programmed bytes per written byte in thousandths, an integer so `no_std` targets need no floats.
- **The wear budget left.** `blocks_left(endurance_cycles)` turns the chip's rated cycles into blocks the caller may still write, at the erases per block seen so far: the number an application compares with its expected lifetime.
- **Budgets.** The layer's own tests hold 512-byte-sector random writes to 1.4x and one hot block among cold ones to 1.6x (smaller sectors pay the journal's 48 bytes per write more; 4 KiB sectors pay about a tenth as much). The engine's end-to-end amplification on the QEMU Cortex-M3 run (bytes programmed per byte of keys and values, 300 keys in one commit then twenty one-key commits) is reported as `flash_write_amplification_milli` with `flash_erased_sectors`, and budgeted in `benchmarks/footprint.txt` at 128x and 512 sectors as first ceilings, to be tightened in the weekly pass once measured (D354).
- **Battery-aware work is the checkpoint.** The engine's background writing is the automatic checkpoint, which rewrites logged pages in place. `Database::set_power_source` stretches it fourfold on a battery and holds it on a low battery (20% or less, or the battery saver) until the log would outgrow its reused segments; durability is unchanged, only crash replay is longer. Scheduled SQL jobs keep their times, since users set them.
- **Who knows the battery.** `eggdb_platform::power_source` reads Linux and Android sysfs (a mains, USB or wireless supply online wins; a device-scoped battery such as a mouse is ignored) and Windows `GetSystemPowerStatus`; other systems answer `None`. `eggdb serve` polls it every minute. Mobile apps pass their platform's own state through `eggdb_set_power_source`, since iOS gives battery state only through UIKit.
