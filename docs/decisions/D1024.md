## D1024 Replication wired into the engine and `eggdb serve` — Decided by the code-only session (2026-11-13, D355); roadmap M14 (D18, D209, D1019); data-critical (D35)

- **The commit hook.** `Database::start_primary` turns on capture: from then on each write transaction records the
  keys it changed, as the change archive does (D1019), and its commit writes the batch's term and sequence under a
  reserved engine key (`\xffCOL\x00\x7freplication`) in the same transaction, then hands the changed keys with their
  new stored bytes to `eggdb-replication`'s `Primary` as the next batch. Term and sequence therefore survive a
  restart exactly as the commits do, and a database without a primary pays nothing (D6).
- **Applying.** A replica applies each batch through `Database::apply_batch`: every change and the batch's position
  in one write transaction, raw keys as the primary stored them, so every model replicates at once (D209). Its
  position read back at start (`replication_position`) tells the primary where to continue.
- **Transport.** `eggdb serve --replication-listen <address>` serves replicas; `eggdb serve --replica-of
  <host:port>` follows a primary, serving clients read-only. Frames are a length, a type and a payload: challenge,
  hello (user, proof, replica id, position), welcome (term), batch (`encode_batch`), acknowledgement, heartbeat,
  refusal. The replica proves the server's password with HMAC-SHA256 of a fresh 32-byte challenge keyed with the
  password's SHA-256, which both servers already hold, so the password never crosses the wire, and a failed proof
  waits 250 ms. Off the local machine the stream is TLS: the primary's own certificate, and on the replica a CA
  given with `--replica-of-ca`.
- **Synchronous commits.** With `--synchronous-replicas <n>` (`ReplicationMode::Synchronous`), a commit returns once
  `n` replicas acknowledged its batch, waiting at most the write timeout; past it the commit stays (it is durable on
  the primary, as PostgreSQL's canceled synchronous wait leaves it) and the client gets an error saying so.
- **Limits of this slice.** The
  primary keeps its last 100,000 batches in memory; a replica that falls further behind, or whose history diverged
  (an old primary rejoining), is told to copy again from a backup, and stops following rather than apply a history
  on top of another. Initial copy from a snapshot, failover, fencing and cascading replicas are the next items of
  M14.
