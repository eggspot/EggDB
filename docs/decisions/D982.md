## D982 Word segmentation for Chinese, Japanese, Korean and Thai — Decided by the code-only session (2026-11-13, D355); roadmap M8, word segmentation (D30, D195)

- `eggdb_search::segmented_words` cuts runs of Chinese, Japanese and Korean characters, and runs of Thai letters,
  into overlapping two-character pieces (`東京都` gives `東京` and `京都`), as Lucene's CJK bigram filter and
  Elasticsearch's `cjk` analyzer do. A run of one character stays whole; other text splits as `words` splits it.
- Thai vowel and tone marks written over or under a letter stay with that letter, and the Japanese long-vowel mark
  `ー` stays with the character before it, so no piece starts with a mark.
- New text search configurations `cjk` and `thai` lower case and segment this way; `to_tsvector`, `plainto_tsquery`,
  `phraseto_tsquery` and `websearch_to_tsquery` use the configuration's segmentation, and a `to_tsquery` word that
  segments into several pieces becomes a phrase of them (`東京都` is `'東京' <-> '京都'`), so a word is found
  inside text written without spaces.
- The other configurations keep PostgreSQL's behaviour, where a run of such characters is one word.
- Elasticsearch-compatible analyzers `cjk` and `thai`, and custom analyzers with the `cjk`, `thai` or
  `icu_tokenizer` tokenizer, segment the same way, at index and at query time.
- Pairs rather than a dictionary: they need no word list to ship (D6) and find every word at the cost of some
  extra matches. Dictionary-based segmentation (longest match against a word list) is left for later.
