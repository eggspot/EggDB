## D656 A French stemmer and the french text-search configuration — Decided by the code-only session (2026-11-12, D355); roadmap M8, D30, D650

PostgreSQL's `french` configuration completes the larger Western European languages. Snowball's
French algorithm is the longest of them.

- Preparation marks `u` after `q`, `u`/`i` between vowels, and `y` next to a vowel, so they count
  as consonants.
- RV starts after the third letter when the word starts with two vowels, after `par`, `col` or
  `tap`, or else after the first vowel that is not the first letter. R1 and R2 are computed as
  usual.
- Step 1 handles the standard suffixes with Snowball's conditions:
  - `ance … istes` in R2;
  - `atrice`/`ateur`/`ation`, then `ic` (or `iqU`);
  - `logie` → `log`, `usion`/`ution` → `u`, `ence` → `ent`;
  - `ement` in RV, then `iv` (and `at`), `eus` (→ `eux` in R1), `abl`/`iqU`, or `ièr` → `i`;
  - `ité`, then `abil` (→ `abl`), `ic` (→ `iqU`) or `iv`;
  - `if`/`ive`, then `at` (and `ic`);
  - `eaux` → `eau`, `aux` → `al` in R1;
  - `euse` (→ `eux` in R1);
  - `issement` after a non-vowel in R1;
  - `amment` → `ant`, `emment` → `ent`;
  - `ment`/`ments` after a vowel in RV.
- Step 2 runs when step 1 removed nothing, or when it found `amment`, `emment` or `ment(s)`:
  - first the `i`-verb endings after a non-vowel in RV;
  - otherwise the other verb endings in RV: `ions` only in R2, and the `a`-forms with an `e`
    before them.
- When a step changed the word, a final `Y` becomes `i` and `ç` becomes `c`. Otherwise the
  residual step runs: `s` (not after `a i o u è s`), then `ion` in R2 after `s`/`t`, `ier` →
  `i`, `e`, and `ë` after `gu`.
- `enn onn ett ell eill` lose their last letter. An `é`/`è` before the final non-vowels becomes
  `e`. The marks are lowered at the end.
- Snowball's French stop words are dropped. `Config::French` is named `french`.
- Test: `continuellement` → `continuel`, `pommes` → `pomm`, `nationale` → `national`. Snowball's
  vocabulary file should be checked when the build runs, since this is the algorithm most likely
  to hide a slip.
