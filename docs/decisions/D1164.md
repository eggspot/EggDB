## D1164 Large HTTP request bodies handed to the handler as they arrive, first for S3 `PutObject` — Decided by the code-only session (2026-11-14, D355); roadmap M6a (D325, D188, D307)

- **The hook.** D325 left "handing a large request body to its handler as it arrives instead of whole".
  `eggdb_http::Limits::with_body_streamer` takes a `BodyStreamer`:
  - `takes(head)` chooses requests from their head alone;
  - `answer(head, body)` reads the body through `std::io::Read`, piece by piece from the connection.
- **What the reader does.** Content-Length and chunked framing are removed (chunk lines and trailers are checked
  as for whole bodies), `100 Continue` is sent first when asked, and the connection holds only the piece being
  read.
  - The listener's `max_body_bytes` does not apply to these requests: the streamer bounds the body itself, as
    it never holds it whole.
  - What the answer leaves unread is read and dropped when it is at most 64 KiB, so the connection serves its
    next request. A longer rest, or an error, closes the connection after the answer.
  - A rate-limited streamed request is answered `429` and its connection closed.
  - A request whose content coding is to be decoded keeps the whole-body path.
- **Reader refactor.** `RequestReader::next_request` is now `next_head` plus `read_body`.
- **S3 `PutObject` of 1 MiB or more** (or chunked) goes to the S3 endpoint's streamer:
  - The signature is checked with the new `sigv4::verify_stated_payload`, which trusts the stated
    `x-amz-content-sha256` and leaves the body to the caller. A request stating no hash is `501`, as its
    signature covers a body not read yet. SigV2 query signatures do not cover the body.
  - `Store::put_object_streamed` writes each 256 KiB piece under a staging prefix unique to the upload, in a
    short transaction of its own, so a slow upload never holds the writer. MD5 (the new streaming
    `eggdb_crypto::Md5`) and SHA-256 are computed on the way.
  - One final transaction moves the pieces under the object and writes its record, replacing any earlier object
    atomically.
  - A body that ends early (`IncompleteBody`), passes 1 GiB (`EntityTooLarge`), or whose Content-MD5
    (`BadDigest`) or stated SHA-256 (`XAmzContentSHA256Mismatch`) differs leaves no staged piece and no object
    behind.
  - Objects up to the store's 1 GiB now upload in one `PUT`, where the whole-body path stopped at 64 MiB.
- **Tests.**
  - `eggdb-http`: a 300,000-byte body and a chunked one reach the streamer in pieces past a 1 KiB body limit, and
    the same connection then serves an ordinary request.
  - `eggdb-crypto`: MD5 in pieces equals MD5 whole at every cut.
  - `eggdb-s3`: a 3 MiB object streamed as it arrives reads back whole with its MD5 ETag, and a wrong
    Content-MD5 leaves nothing.
- **Later.** Other listeners (Elasticsearch `_bulk`, the REST API's bulk inserts) can take the same hook as
  they gain row-at-a-time writers.

**Status:** code written, unbuilt (code-only mode, D355).
