## D590 Geo searches over nine geohash cells — Decided by the code-only session (2026-11-12, D355); roadmap M7, D193, D330

`GEOSEARCH`, `GEORADIUS` and their store forms measured the distance to every member of the set.
Redis looks only at the geohash cell holding the center and its eight neighbours.

- `geohash::search_ranges` takes the search area's bounding box (half width and half height in
  meters, with a 1% margin; for a radius both are the radius) and starts from Redis's step estimate
  for the area's radius (one fewer step above 66° latitude, two fewer above 80°).
- It then lowers the step until the three-by-three block of cells around the center covers the
  bounding box, so no member inside the area can fall outside the block.
- Each cell is the score range `[hash << 2(26 - step), (hash + 1) << 2(26 - step))`. The ranges are
  merged, and members are picked by binary search over the score-ordered members.
- The chosen members keep their score order, so `ANY`, `COUNT` and the unordered replies stay as
  before. Only the chosen members are decoded and measured.
- A box that reaches a pole or the antimeridian scans every member as before, as do an area that
  even step 1 cannot cover and a center outside the indexable range.
- A test draws 200 centers with random radii and boxes, and 200 points around each. Every point
  the full scan would return must lie in one of the ranges.

The set is still loaded whole (per-element storage is its own item), so the saving is the distance
work, not the read.
