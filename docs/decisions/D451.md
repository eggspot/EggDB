## D451 More NLS languages and territories — Decided by the code-only session (2026-11-11, D355); roadmap M10 (item 42b)

- `NLS_LANGUAGE` and `NLS_DATE_LANGUAGE` take six more languages beside the seven of D298:
  SWEDISH, DANISH, NORWEGIAN, FINNISH, POLISH and CATALAN, with their month and day names and
  abbreviations as those languages write them (upper case, as Oracle shows them). Oracle's own
  abbreviation tables may differ in places (Polish day abbreviations especially); `TO_CHAR` and
  `TO_DATE` use whatever the table says, both ways.
- `NLS_TERRITORY` takes 14 territories instead of only AMERICA: UNITED KINGDOM, GERMANY, AUSTRIA,
  FRANCE, ITALY, SPAIN, THE NETHERLANDS, SWEDEN, DENMARK, NORWAY, FINLAND, POLAND and BRAZIL. As in
  Oracle, setting one resets what the territory implies: the date format (`DD.MM.RR` in Germany,
  `RRRR-MM-DD` in Sweden, ...), the time-stamp formats built on it with a 24-hour clock, the local
  currency symbol, the ISO currency where the ISO table has the territory, and the decimal
  character and group separator (`,` and `.` in Germany, `,` and a space in France); `NLS_NUMERIC_
  CHARACTERS` set afterwards still overrides them. Other territories stay ORA-12705. The formats
  come from Oracle's documented defaults where known and the countries' usual forms otherwise.
- Left: the remaining languages and territories, and `NLS_SORT`/`NLS_COMP` linguistic ordering.
