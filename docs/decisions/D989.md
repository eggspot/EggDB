## D989 MongoDB collations in writes and `aggregate` — Decided by the code-only session (2026-11-13, D355); roadmap M9, collations (D197, D738)

- `update` and `delete` statements and `findAndModify` take a `collation`. The documents its filter matches under the
  collation (D738's keys) are found first, the first only for a single-document write (in `findAndModify`'s `sort`
  order), and the write then runs on a filter of their `_id`s, so updates, `$`-free operators, deletes and upserts keep
  their one implementation.
- When nothing matches under the collation, an upsert builds its new document from the statement's own filter, as
  MongoDB does; a delete deletes nothing.
- `aggregate` with a `collation` reads its input through the collation: the leading `$match` (or every document) is
  matched by collation keys. Later `$sort`, `$group` and `$match` stages still compare binary strings.
- Like D738, a collated write reads the whole collection, since no index carries collation keys yet.
