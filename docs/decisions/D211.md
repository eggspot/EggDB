## D211 Raft in our own eggdb-consensus, as a deterministic message-driven state machine — Decided by the build loop (by judgement, 2026-10-31); refines D4, D18 and D20

Part 4.M15-1 asks for a three-node cluster that survives losing a node, on a replicated log,
checked by deterministic simulation of partitions and crashes.

- **Raft as the paper states it** (Ongaro and Ousterhout): randomized election timeouts, votes
  only for candidates whose log is at least as up to date, appends checked against the previous
  entry's term with conflicting suffixes truncated, and commitment by majority only for entries
  of the leader's own term (a new leader appends a no-op to commit what came before).
- **No I/O inside.** A `Node` takes messages and ticks and returns messages; persistence and the
  network belong to the caller. The same code runs in `eggdb serve` later and in tests, where a
  seeded simulator drops, delays and partitions messages and crashes and restarts nodes, and
  checks after every round that no term has two leaders and no index commits two commands, then
  that the healed cluster commits a new proposal everywhere.
- **What the log carries is the replication batches of D209**: a committed entry is a batch the
  state machine applies, so failover in a cluster is Raft's election rather than D209's
  "most advanced replica" rule.

Left: persisting the term, vote and log in the file's reserved consensus area (D20), snapshots and
log compaction, membership changes (joint consensus), pre-vote and leader leases for reads, the
network transport and `eggdb serve --cluster`, and Jepsen-style tests against processes.
