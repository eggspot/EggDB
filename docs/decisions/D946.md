## D946 Packet loss, duplication and reordering, and acknowledgements with latency, in the simulated network — Decided by the code-only session (2026-11-13, D355); roadmap M1, follow-up of D314

- **Packet faults.** `SimNetwork::packet_loss(per_million)`, `packet_duplication(per_million)` and
  `packet_reordering(per_million, most)` act on each packet between two different hosts, drawn from the network's
  seed, and draw nothing at a rate of zero, so every existing seeded test keeps its sequence.
  - On a connection a lost packet is retransmitted after Linux's retransmission timeout, 200 ms doubling up to 120 s
    for each loss in a row (at most 15), so its bytes and every later byte of that direction arrive that much later,
    still in order; a held-back packet delays the bytes behind it the same way; duplicates are invisible, as a TCP
    receiver drops them. Handshakes are left out, so connects are not slowed.
  - A lost datagram never arrives, a duplicated one arrives twice (the copy with its own latency), and a held-back one
    may be overtaken by later datagrams.
- **Acknowledgements with latency.** `SimNetwork::acknowledgement_latency(true)` makes the acknowledgement of bytes
  that arrived take the latency back from the receiver to the sender (network and host links, drawn from the seed):
  until it arrives the sender's bytes count as unacknowledged, so a cut of the way back in that window starts the
  retransmission timer, as on a real network. It is off by default because the extra draws would move every other
  latency a seeded test draws. A peer that went away after its acknowledgement left still counts as having
  acknowledged, so closing a connection right after reading does not turn into a reset.
- **Checks.** Simulator tests: a lost packet's bytes arrive 804.6 s later (the 15 doubled timeouts) and in order with
  the bytes sent after them; a held-back packet delays without reordering; an acknowledgement still on its way when
  the way back is cut times the sender out only with acknowledgement latency on; lost datagrams never arrive,
  duplicated ones arrive twice, and a held-back datagram is overtaken.
