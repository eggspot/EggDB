## D634 DBMS_SCHEDULER chains — Decided by the code-only session (2026-11-12, D355); roadmap M10, D524, D625

Oracle batch loads are often a chain: extract, then transform and validate side by side, then
publish only when both succeeded, and alert on failure. `DBMS_SCHEDULER.CREATE_CHAIN` with its
steps and rules is how they are written, and a `CHAIN` job runs them. D625 left chains out.

- `CREATE_CHAIN`, `DROP_CHAIN`, `DEFINE_CHAIN_STEP(chain, step, program)`, `DROP_CHAIN_STEP`,
  `DEFINE_CHAIN_RULE(chain, condition, action, rule_name)` (a rule without a name gets
  `RULE$n`), `DROP_CHAIN_RULE` and `RUN_CHAIN(chain_name, job_name, start_steps)`. Chains,
  steps and rules live in three internal tables beside the programs. `ENABLE` and `DISABLE`
  address a chain when no job has the name; a chain starts disabled, as Oracle's does.
- Conditions take Oracle's simple syntax: `TRUE`, `FALSE`, `step COMPLETED|SUCCEEDED|FAILED|
  NOT_STARTED`, with `AND`, `OR`, `NOT` and parentheses. `STOPPED`, `RUNNING`, `PAUSED` and
  `STALLED` never hold, since steps run to their end one after another. Actions are
  `START step[, step ...]` and `END [n]`. Both are checked when the rule is defined (ORA-27452).
  The SQL-style syntax (`:step.state = 'SUCCEEDED'`) is not read yet.
- Running a chain: every step starts as not started. Unless `start_steps` names the first
  steps, the rules are evaluated in rule-name order; each true rule's `START` queues the steps
  that have not started, and an `END` stops the chain. The queued steps run, each its program's
  block, and finish as succeeded or failed (a failing step does not fail the chain). Then the
  rules are evaluated again, until a round starts nothing or a rule ends the chain. Every round
  starts at least one new step, so a chain runs at most as many rounds as it has steps.
- `CREATE_JOB(job_type => 'CHAIN', job_action => 'chain')` checks that the chain exists; such a
  job, run by `RUN_JOB` or by the scheduler, runs `RUN_CHAIN` on it.
- Not done: steps that are chains or events, step-level `SET_ATTRIBUTE` options (skip, pause,
  restart on failure), `ALTER_RUNNING_CHAIN`, `evaluation_interval`, and the chain dictionary
  views (`ALL_SCHEDULER_CHAINS`, `_CHAIN_STEPS`, `_CHAIN_RULES`, `_RUNNING_CHAINS`).
- Tests: the condition and action parsers' tests, and an Oracle test whose chain runs `a`, then
  `b` and a failing `f`, then `c` because `f` failed, and never the step reserved for `f`'s
  success.
