## D607 The Leiden community algorithm — Decided by the code-only session (2026-11-12, D355); roadmap M13, D248, D436

D436 wrote Louvain and left Leiden. Louvain can return a community whose parts are not connected
to each other. Leiden adds a refinement step that rules this out, which is why GDS and igraph users
reach for it.

- `CALL eggdb.leiden([maxLevels])` yields `nodeId` and `communityId`, like `eggdb.louvain`, and
  reads relationships without direction and weight 1.
- Each level runs Louvain's local moves, then the refinement:
  - Within each community, every node starts as its own sub-community.
  - A node still alone joins the neighbouring sub-community of the same community with the
    largest positive modularity gain, but only when it is well connected to its community: its
    links into the community are at least `degree × (community degree − degree) / 2m`.
- The level is then aggregated by the refined sub-communities, not by the communities, so every
  community that comes out is connected.
- The method is deterministic: nodes are visited in id order and the best gain wins, with no
  random choice. A community is named by its smallest node id.
- When the refinement merges nothing, the communities of that level are taken as the result and
  the levels stop, as the next level would repeat the same moves.
- Differences from the published method:
  - each new level's local moves start from single nodes, not from the communities carried down;
  - there is no randomness in the refinement;
  - modularity's resolution is fixed at 1.
- The test that splits two triangles joined by one edge into two communities now checks Leiden
  too.

Not done: learned embeddings (node2vec, GraphSAGE), and a resolution parameter.
