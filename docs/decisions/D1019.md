## D1019 Continuous change archiving and restore to any commit — Decided by the code-only session (2026-11-13, D355); roadmap M14 (D24, D208); data-critical (D35)

- D208 left "continuous log shipping and replay to any commit". Shipping the write-ahead log itself cannot work
  with EggDB's snapshots: a backup is a logical copy whose pages are numbered afresh, so the log's page images
  mean nothing to it, and the log recycles its segments at each checkpoint. The archive is therefore logical: each
  commit's changed keys with their new stored bytes (or their deletion), the same unit replication will ship
  (D209), kept outside the file (format in `docs/format.md`, "Change archive").
- Order of writes: the changes are appended and synced before the storage commit, and a `committed` record with the
  stamp after it (`aborted` when the commit fails). Opening settles a commit left unanswered by comparing the
  database's last stamp with the last stamp the archive knows; commits made while the archive was not kept are
  marked as a gap. A failed append makes the database refuse further commits until it is reopened, so no commit
  goes unarchived without a mark. A failed commit after its changes were archived is marked `aborted`; if the
  failed commit did reach the disk, the next open sees the database ahead and marks a gap rather than guess.
- `eggdb backup` records each copy's commit stamp in `manifest.jsonl`; `eggdb restore <new> <directory> --at
  <time>` restores the newest copy at or before the time and replays `<directory>/changes` from the copy's stamp
  to the last commit at or before the time, each archived commit as one commit; a replay that would cross a gap,
  or an archive that begins after the copy, is refused before anything is written. The library exposes
  `Options::archive_changes_to`, `read_change_archive` and `replay_changes`.
- Data-critical by D35: the PR that builds this gets its own review pass on data safety, and the format joins the
  compatibility tests. Left: the deterministic simulator test of restoring to a stamp under crashes, archives in
  S3-compatible buckets, and pruning old segments with retention.
