## D1331 PostgreSQL's geometric types — Decided by the code-only session (2026-11-25, D355); roadmap "PostgreSQL's network address, geometric ... types" (D19)

- **What users get.** `point`, `line`, `lseg`, `box`, `path`, `polygon` and `circle` columns, casts and `type 'literal'` constants in PostgreSQL's dialect, read in every form PostgreSQL reads (`(1,2)` or `1,2`; a box from any two corners, kept as upper right then lower left; open `[...]` and closed `(...)` paths; `{A,B,C}` lines or a line through two points; `<(x,y),r>` circles) and printed as PostgreSQL prints them. Operators: `<->` (distance between points, a point and any shape, circles, boxes' centers and polygons), `@>` and `<@` (boxes, circles and polygons containing points and shapes), `&&` (overlap), `<<` and `>>` (strictly left or right), `~=` (same shape) and `+`/`-` with a point (moving points, boxes, paths and circles). Functions: `area`, `center`, `radius`, `diameter`, `height`, `width`, `length`, `npoints`, `isclosed`, `isopen`, `popen`, `pclose`, `slope`, `bound_box`, `diagonal`, and the constructors and conversions `point`, `box`, `circle`, `lseg`, `line`, `path` and `polygon` (`polygon(n, circle)` included). OIDs 600–604, 628 and 718 in text and binary form, with `pg_type` rows for them and their arrays.
- **How.** A `DefinedValue::Geometric` keeps the type, whether a path is closed, and the numbers as `f64`; stored rows tag each type (42 to 48) and keep the numbers' exact bits. Sorting and equality follow PostgreSQL's B-tree-less types as closely as a total order allows: boxes and circles by area (PostgreSQL's `box = box` compares areas), segments by length, paths by their number of points, the others by their coordinates.
- **Fixed on the way.** D1328 and D1329 had added `pg_type` rows out of OID order, which breaks the binary search over `BUILTIN_TYPES`; the table is sorted again and every row added here is in place.
- **Left.** GiST and SP-GiST indexes on these types, `##` (closest point), `?#`, `?-`, `?|`, `?-|`, `?||`, `@-@` and `@@` as operators, `*` and `/` on points (rotation and scaling), and `path + path`.
- **Proof.** Unit tests for every input form and output, measures, distances, containment, overlap, moving, and the stored and binary forms; a session test stores four shapes per row and queries containment, ordered distance, areas, overlap and `~=`.

**Status:** code written, unbuilt (code-only mode, D355).
