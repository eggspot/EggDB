## D1135 Joules per query, idle wake-ups and serve's time to its first line — Decided by the code-only session (2026-11-14, D355); roadmap M4 (D14, D44, D230, D252, D354)

- **Where they are measured.** `scripts/footprint.sh`, the Saturday performance pass's script (D354), now also
  measures three things.
  - **Serve's time to its first line**, from launch to the first byte of output, for both systems. Target 250 ms,
    well inside D14's "well under a second".
  - **Idle wake-ups** of `eggdb serve` on Linux: voluntary plus involuntary context switches over 20 seconds of
    idleness after it settles, from `/proc/<pid>/status`, given per minute. Target 60, at most one a second, so an
    idle server lets a laptop's or phone's processor sleep. Each wake-up the engine schedules (checkpoints, power
    followers, expiry sweeps) has to fit inside it.
  - **Joules per query**, where the processor's RAPL energy counter can be read (bare-metal Linux;
    `/sys/class/powercap/intel-rapl:0/energy_uj`). It covers two workloads of 20,000 statements through `eggdb sql`:
    inserts in one transaction, and point selects by primary key. Each is given as millijoules per 1,000 queries,
    the package's energy over the run divided by the statements. CI's virtual machines expose no counter, so there
    the lines are skipped rather than failed.
- **Reported, then gated.** Wake-ups and first-line time have targets in `benchmarks/footprint.txt` now. The energy
  lines print "no target yet" until the first pass on hardware with a readable counter sets them (D44's dedicated
  benchmark hardware is the owner's "Needs the owner" item). From then on they gate like every other footprint line.
