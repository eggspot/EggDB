## D596 Tailable cursors on capped collections — Decided by the code-only session (2026-11-12, D355); roadmap M9, D197, D532, D588

Capped collections exist so that something can follow them: a log tailer, a job queue polled with
`tailable: true, awaitData: true`, or Mongoose's `tailable()`. D532 wrote capped collections and
D588 wrote the change log, which this builds on.

- `find` with `tailable: true` is allowed only on a capped collection, and only without `sort`,
  `limit` or `singleBatch`. Otherwise it answers `BadValue`, with MongoDB's "tailable cursor
  requested on non capped collection" message for the first case.
- The find takes the change log position before it reads. Its first batch comes in natural
  (insertion) order, and the cursor stays open even when that batch is empty.
- The cursor keeps a `Tail`: the position, filter, projection and `awaitData` flag, plus the
  `_id`s of the first result. Those ids cover an insert that the log recorded after the
  position but before the read finished, so it is not returned twice.
- `getMore` first returns what the first batch left. After that, it reads the change log for
  insert events into that collection, keeps the documents the filter matches, and applies the
  projection.
  - With `awaitData` it waits up to `maxTimeMS` (one second when absent, at most ten), polling
    every 100 ms like change streams. Without it, it answers at once.
  - The lock on the cursors is released while it waits, and the cursor id stays the same.
- An insert that the change log no longer keeps (past `KEPT_CHANGES`) is not seen. A cursor idle
  for ten minutes closes, as every cursor does.

Not done: tailing with `OP_QUERY`'s tailable flags (legacy drivers), and GridFS's own checks.
