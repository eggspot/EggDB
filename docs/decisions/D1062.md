## D1062 JMS clients over AMQP 1.0 — Decided by the code-only session (2026-11-13, D355); roadmap M16 (D33, D217)

- **One stored form.** AMQP 1.0 messages are now stored the way AMQP 0-9-1 stores them: exchange, routing key,
  basic properties and body.
  - Before this, AMQP 1.0 stored only the body bytes. Properties were lost, and a 0-9-1 message read by an
    AMQP 1.0 receiver showed the 0-9-1 framing as its body.
  - The properties, application properties, message annotations and footer of an AMQP 1.0 message are kept
    byte for byte in `x-amqp-1.0-properties`, `-app-properties`, `-message-annotations` and `-footer` headers.
  - They are also mapped to the nearest 0-9-1 fields:
    - `message-id` to `message_id`, `subject` to `type` (JMSType), `reply-to`, `correlation-id`, and
      `content-type` / `content-encoding`;
    - `creation-time` to `timestamp`, the header's durability to `delivery_mode`, priority to `priority`, and
      TTL to `expiration`;
    - each application property and annotation to a header.
  - An AMQP 1.0 receiver gets the kept sections back unchanged. A message published over 0-9-1 gets sections
    built from its properties.
  - An `amqp-value` string body, which is how JMS sends a TextMessage, is stored as UTF-8 text and marked
    `x-amqp-1.0-body-kind: string`, so 0-9-1 consumers read text. Other value and sequence bodies are kept as
    their sections.
  - The headers' naming follows the convention RabbitMQ's documentation describes for its AMQP 1.0 plugin.
- **Topics.** A link names a topic with the `topic` or `temporary-topic` capability, or with a `/topic/` or
  `topic://` address.
  - A sender's messages go to `amq.topic`, with the topic as the routing key, so `*` and `#` patterns work in
    subscriptions.
  - A receiver gets its own subscription queue bound to the topic.
  - The queue is durable when the source is durable or its expiry policy is `never` (a JMS durable
    subscription). It is named `subscription.<container-id>.<link name>`, or `subscription.shared.<name>` with
    the `shared` capability.
  - A durable subscription queue lives until the client detaches with `closed` (JMS `unsubscribe`, which
    re-attaches by link name with no source). Other subscription queues end with their link or connection.
- **The anonymous relay.** `open` offers `ANONYMOUS-RELAY` and `SHARED-SUBS`. A sender link with no target
  address routes each message by its `to` and its `x-opt-jms-dest` annotation (topics through `amq.topic`;
  queues are declared as they are named).
- **Selectors.** A source's `apache.org:selector-filter:string` filter (descriptor `0x468C00000004`) is read
  with the JMS selector parser.
  - The parser moved from `eggdb-stomp` to `eggdb_queues::selector`, with typed values, so STOMP and AMQP
    share one. STOMP passes its headers as text, as before.
  - The selector sees `JMSDeliveryMode`, `JMSPriority`, `JMSMessageID`, `JMSTimestamp`, `JMSCorrelationID`,
    `JMSType` and the message's properties.
  - A subscription queue drops the messages its selector refuses.
  - A shared queue leaves refused messages for other receivers. Each pass scans at most the first 1,000
    visible messages.
  - Left out: arithmetic in selectors.
- **Transactions.** A coordinator target (`0x30`) takes `declare` (answered with `declared` and an 8-byte
  id) and `discharge`.
  - A transfer with a `transactional-state` is staged. A disposition with one stages the receiver's
    outcome.
  - Commit applies both. Rollback drops the staged sends and returns the staged messages to their queues as
    redelivered, which JMS rollback requires.
  - Limits: at most 64 open transactions per connection, and 100,000 items or 64 MiB per transaction.
    Transactions still open when the connection ends are rolled back.
- Learned from: the AMQP 1.0 specification (messaging and transactions), the AMQP JMS Mapping working draft,
  and the Apache Qpid JMS client's public documentation. No client or broker source was read.
