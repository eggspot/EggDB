## D246 Write-once retention, legal holds and waiting erasure in the `eggdb_retention` extension — Decided by the build loop (by judgement, 2026-10-31); refines D26 and D53

Track 4.X-18 asks for legal hold over crypto-shredding, WORM retention and ransomware-proof
backups, records schedules, eDiscovery export, residency per row, in-country keys, operator
access limits and cross-border transfer logs. This slice does retention, holds and erasure.

- **Per table, enforced by a trigger.** `retain` and `place_hold` record the rule in the
  extension's tables and put one `BEFORE UPDATE OR DELETE` trigger on the table. A held table
  refuses every change; a retained table refuses every update, and a delete until the row's time
  column plus the period has passed. A row whose time column is NULL is kept.
- **Erasure waits, as D53 says.** An erasure request is recorded first. It runs at once when
  nothing holds the table; otherwise it waits with the reason, and `process_erasures()` carries
  it out once the hold is lifted or the retention ends. The record of the request, its wait and
  its result stays.
- **Rules, not yet tamper-proof.** A trigger does not stop `TRUNCATE`, `DROP TABLE`, or a role
  that drops the trigger. The docs say to keep those rights with administrators. Engine-level
  WORM that also binds them, and immutable backups, come with the storage work.

Left: holds over a subset of rows, holds on crypto-shredding (D26), engine-level WORM and
immutable backups, records schedules, eDiscovery export, residency per row, in-country keys,
operator access limits and transfer logs. Compliance claims stay with the owner.
