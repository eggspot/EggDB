## D447 ASOF joins — Decided by the code-only session (2026-11-11, D355); roadmap M11

- The PostgreSQL dialect takes DuckDB's `ASOF [LEFT [OUTER]] JOIN table [alias] ON equalities AND
  one inequality`: for each row on the left, the one row of `table` that satisfies the
  equalities and lies nearest on the inequality's side (`left.ts >= right.ts` takes the latest
  right row at or before the left time; `<=` the earliest at or after; `>`/`<` strictly).
- It is a parser rewrite (`parser/asof.rs`) onto machinery that exists: a `LATERAL` subquery
  `(SELECT * FROM table alias WHERE <the ON condition> ORDER BY <the right side of the
  inequality> DESC|ASC LIMIT 1)`, cross-joined for `ASOF JOIN` (left rows without a match go) and
  left-joined on `true` for `ASOF LEFT JOIN` (they stay with NULLs). The inequality is the one
  conjunct comparing an expression of the right table with one that doesn't use it; none, or
  more than one, is a syntax error. `asof` stays usable as an alias except right before `JOIN`
  or `LEFT`.
- Each left row runs one ordered, limited lookup, so the join is as fast as the right table's
  index on the equality and time columns allows; a merge-based ASOF join over sorted inputs and
  window joins are left, with ClickHouse's `ASOF JOIN ... USING` form.
