## D1192 Table owners — Decided by the code-only session (2026-11-17, D355); roadmap M6b (D183); security-critical (D35)

- **What users get.** `ALTER TABLE ... OWNER TO <role>` (PostgreSQL dialect) makes a stored role the table's owner, as `pg_dump` scripts and administrators write it: the owner, and the roles that belong to it, hold every privilege on the table, may grant them on (D1189), and are not stopped by a denial, as in PostgreSQL. `pg_class.relowner` names the owner, and `relacl` lists it first and as the grantor of the table's grants.
- **Storage.** One key space (`OWNER_SPACE` 0x1E) maps a table to its owner's name; no entry means the session's superuser owns it, as before. Renaming a table moves its owner, and with it the grant options of D1189 and the denials (which a rename did not move before); dropping it forgets them. A name that is no stored role (`OWNER TO postgres` in a dump) leaves the owner as it was, and a superuser role as owner clears the entry, so restores keep working.
- **Left of the roadmap item.** Non-superusers still cannot create tables (D827), so ownership comes only from `ALTER ... OWNER TO`; owners of views, sequences, schemas, types and routines; `REASSIGN OWNED` and `DROP OWNED`; recording each grant's grantor for `REVOKE ... CASCADE`; `FORCE ROW LEVEL SECURITY`, which needs owners to be exempt by default first.
- **Proof.** A unit test makes a role a table's owner, reads `relowner`, inserts, deletes, grants on and truncates after a rename as that role, and is refused on a table it does not own; a dump's `OWNER TO postgres` is accepted.
- **Security review.** As a D35 change it needs a security-only review pass once built: that ownership cannot be taken by a non-superuser, and that a rename or drop never leaves an owner behind for a later table of the same name.

**Status:** code written, unbuilt (code-only mode, D355).
