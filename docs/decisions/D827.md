## D827 `SET ROLE` — Decided by the code-only session (2026-11-12, D355); roadmap M6b, D183

- `SET [SESSION] ROLE name` switches the role whose privileges are checked to `name` when the login is a superuser
  or a member of `name` (directly or through other roles, `role_closure`); otherwise `42501`, and `22023` when the
  role does not exist. A superuser target makes the session a superuser; any other is checked as that role. The
  login's own state is kept, and `SET ROLE NONE`, `RESET ROLE` or `SET ROLE <login>` return to it. Membership is
  judged by the login, so roles can be switched between freely, as PostgreSQL does.
- `current_user` (and `current_role`, `user`) shows the chosen role, `session_user` the login, through the `role`
  setting, which `SHOW role` reads.
- `SET ROLE` to another role was refused with `0A000` before; this replaces that. `SET SESSION AUTHORIZATION` to
  another user is still refused.
- Left: object owners, `WITH GRANT OPTION`, and schema, sequence and routine privileges.
