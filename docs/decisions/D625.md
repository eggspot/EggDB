## D625 DBMS_SCHEDULER programs and named schedules — Decided by the code-only session (2026-11-12, D355); roadmap M10, D524

Oracle teams define what runs (a program) and when (a schedule) once, and point many jobs at
them: `CREATE_JOB(job_name => 'nightly_load', program_name => 'load_program', schedule_name =>
'nightly')`. D524 took only inline job types, actions and intervals.

- `CREATE_PROGRAM(program_name, program_type, program_action, number_of_arguments, enabled,
  comments)` keeps a `PLSQL_BLOCK` or `STORED_PROCEDURE` program, and `DROP_PROGRAM` removes it.
- `CREATE_SCHEDULE(schedule_name, start_date, repeat_interval, end_date, comments)` keeps a
  schedule, its interval checked by the same calendaring parser as jobs. `DROP_SCHEDULE` removes
  it.
- Names are upper-cased like job names, and both are kept in internal tables beside the jobs.
  Errors use Oracle's codes: ORA-27477 already exists, ORA-27476 does not exist, ORA-27465 a bad
  interval.
- `CREATE_JOB` takes `program_name` and `schedule_name` by name.
  - The program supplies the job type and action when the call gives none.
  - The schedule supplies the start date, interval and end date when the call gives none.
  - A named program or schedule that does not exist refuses the job.
- Differences from Oracle:
  - the job copies its program and schedule when it is created, where Oracle follows later
    changes to them;
  - dropping a program or schedule that jobs use is allowed, and the jobs keep their copies;
  - `ENABLE` and `DISABLE` still address jobs only.
- An Oracle test builds a program and a schedule, runs a job made from them, and checks the
  refusals.
