## D624 SQL Server Agent step flow and retries — Decided by the code-only session (2026-11-12, D355); roadmap M10, D523, D623

D523 ran a job's steps in order and stopped at the first failure. Real Agent jobs route between
steps: "on failure go to the cleanup step", "quit reporting success after the last step", "retry
the flaky extract twice". Without that, a job whose failure branch should notify and continue
ended early instead.

- `sp_add_jobstep` accepts `@on_success_action`, `@on_success_step_id`, `@on_fail_action`,
  `@on_fail_step_id`, `@retry_attempts` and `@retry_interval`.
  - Actions are SQL Server's: 1 quit with success, 2 quit with failure, 3 go to the next step,
    4 go to the given step.
  - Other values, and negative retries, are refused with error 14266.
  - The settings are kept per step in a new internal table, `sqlserver_agent_step_flow`. Steps
    without a row keep D523's behaviour: next step on success, quit with failure on failure.
- The runner now walks the steps by these actions.
  - A step is tried `1 + @retry_attempts` times. Each failed attempt before the last is recorded
    in the history with `run_status` 2 (retry), as Agent does, and the retry runs at once rather
    than after `@retry_interval` minutes.
  - Going past the last step ends the job with the last step's outcome. A missing go-to step does
    the same.
  - A walk of more than 1,000 steps stops with an error, so a flow that loops cannot run forever.
- `msdb.dbo.sysjobsteps` (D623) shows the stored flow, with the defaults for steps without one.
- A test runs a job:
  - its first step fails, is retried once, and jumps to step 3;
  - step 2 is skipped;
  - step 3 quits with success;
  - the history and the rows written are checked, and an invalid action is refused.
