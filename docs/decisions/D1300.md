## D1300 PostgreSQL's date_add and date_subtract — Decided by the code-only session (2026-11-18, D355); roadmap M4 PostgreSQL functions (D10)

- **What users get.** PostgreSQL 16's `date_add(timestamptz, interval[, zone])` and `date_subtract(timestamptz, interval[, zone])`: the interval's months and days are added on the calendar of the zone given (the session's time zone without one), so a day across a daylight-saving change is a calendar day, as PostgreSQL defines them.
- **How.** Beside `make_date` in `temporal.rs`: without a zone the instant is shifted as `timestamptz + interval` is; with one, it is moved to that zone's local time, the interval added there, and moved back. MySQL's `DATE_ADD` keeps its own form, which MySQL's parser rewrites before evaluation.
- **Left.** Nothing of its own.
- **Proof.** A test adds a day in UTC and subtracts one in the session's zone.
