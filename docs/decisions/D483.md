## D483 AMQP 0-9-1 `tx` transactions — Decided by the code-only session (2026-11-11, D355); roadmap M16, D214

- `tx.select` makes a channel transactional: its `basic.publish` messages (routed when published)
  and its `basic.ack`, `basic.nack` and `basic.reject` are staged on the channel. `tx.commit`
  applies them in order (the messages to the queues they were routed to that still exist, then the
  settlements) and `tx.rollback` drops them; both answer and start the next transaction, as AMQP
  0-9-1 specifies. A rolled-back acknowledgement leaves its message unacknowledged.
- `tx.commit`/`tx.rollback` on a channel without `tx.select`, and switching between transactions
  and publisher confirms either way, close the channel with `PRECONDITION_FAILED`, as RabbitMQ
  does. A transaction holds at most 10,000 operations and 64 MiB of messages.
- A commit writes each message as its own queue append, so a failure part-way through a commit
  leaves the earlier messages delivered (RabbitMQ gives the same guarantee across queues); a
  single atomic write across queues would need `eggdb-queues` to take a batch over several queues,
  which `send_all` does within one call and a later change can use here.
