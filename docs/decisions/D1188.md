## D1188 SAML sign-in for interactive tools — Decided by the code-only session (2026-11-15, D355); roadmap M6b (D22); security-critical (D35)

- **What users get.** Companies whose identity provider speaks SAML 2.0 (Okta, Entra ID, ADFS, Shibboleth, Keycloak, Google Workspace) sign their people in to EggDB with it. A rule `all saml idp_entity=<provider entity ID> idp_sso=<provider SSO URL> idp_certificate=<provider certificate PEM> sp_entity=<EggDB's entity ID> acs=https://<host>/auth/saml/acs [name_attribute=<attribute>]` turns it on. The user opens `https://<host>/auth/saml/login` on the REST listener, signs in at the provider, and the page EggDB shows after the provider's POST gives a one-time ticket (`eggdb_saml_<64 hex>`) to use as the password in psql, the GUI, DBeaver or any tool, within five minutes, over TLS. Database protocols cannot carry a browser flow, so the ticket bridges the two, as other databases' "external browser" sign-ins do; the role must exist with `LOGIN` and its privileges apply. The ticket works on the PostgreSQL, MySQL and TDS listeners.
- **Checks, written from SAML 2.0 core and bindings, XML Signature 1.1 and Exclusive XML Canonicalization 1.0 in `eggdb_auth::saml`.** The base64 response is parsed by a reader of our own that refuses any DOCTYPE or entity other than the five predefined ones (no XXE, no entity expansion), more than 64 levels, 20 000 elements or 1 MiB. The signature (on the assertion, or on the response holding it) must have exactly one `Reference` to an element by `ID`, that ID must be on exactly one element, the signature must be that element's child, and the transforms must be enveloped-signature and exclusive C14N (its `InclusiveNamespaces` prefix list honoured); the digest (SHA-256 or SHA-512) of the canonical element without the signature must match, and the signature over the canonical `SignedInfo` (RSA PKCS#1 v1.5 with SHA-256 or SHA-512, or ECDSA P-256 with SHA-256) must verify with the configured certificate's key: the key a message carries in `KeyInfo` is never used, and SHA-1 methods are refused. The trusted values are read only from that signed element (exactly one unencrypted `Assertion`), which defeats signature wrapping. Then: status `Success`, the issuer is `idp_entity`, `Conditions` are within their times (three minutes' skew), the audience is `sp_entity`, a bearer `SubjectConfirmation` names `acs` as its recipient and has not expired, and the name comes from `NameID` or `name_attribute`. Encrypted assertions are refused rather than decrypted.
- **Tickets** are 256-bit random, kept in memory as SHA-256 hashes for five minutes (at most 10 000), bound to the role, and gone once redeemed; the REST listener and the protocol listeners share them because `serve` hands both the same rule set.
- **Requests.** The login page sends an `AuthnRequest` with a fresh random ID by the HTTP-Redirect binding (raw DEFLATE in stored blocks, base64, URL-encoded). `InResponseTo` is not matched against sent requests, so unsolicited (IdP-initiated) responses are accepted, as many providers send them.
- **Proof.** Unit tests: canonicalization against hand-checked output (namespaces, sorting, escaping, comments), hostile XML refused, times, tickets (wrong user, reuse, expiry) and the request URL, and a response signed with a P-256 key that is accepted, refused after expiry, refused when its NameID is changed after signing, and refused under another provider's certificate.
- **Security review.** As a D35 change it needs its own security-only review pass once built: canonicalization corner cases against real providers' responses, and the wrapping rules.
- Left: signed `AuthnRequest`s, encrypted assertions, single logout, provider metadata files, and a CI job against a real provider (Keycloak).

**Status:** code written, unbuilt (code-only mode, D355).
