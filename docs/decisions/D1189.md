## D1189 `WITH GRANT OPTION` on table privileges — Decided by the code-only session (2026-11-17, D355); roadmap M6b (D183); security-critical (D35)

- **What users get.** `GRANT SELECT ON audit TO clerk WITH GRANT OPTION` lets `clerk`, and the roles that belong to `clerk`'s roles, grant `SELECT` on `audit` to others, as in PostgreSQL, MySQL and SQL Server. `REVOKE GRANT OPTION FOR SELECT ON audit FROM clerk` takes back only that right, and `REVOKE SELECT` takes both. `has_table_privilege('audit', 'SELECT WITH GRANT OPTION')` answers it, and `relacl` and `\dp` mark each grantable privilege with `*` (`clerk=ar*/ada`).
- **Storage.** The right to grant on lives in its own key space (`GRANT_OPTION_SPACE` 0x1C, keyed like the grants: table, zero byte, grantee), so the grant records keep their format and older files read as before. Dropping a table forgets them with its grants and denials; the access snapshot the catalog answers from loads them.
- **Who may grant.** A session whose role is checked may run `GRANT` or `REVOKE` of table privileges (not column privileges, not role memberships) when it, or a role it belongs to, holds every named privilege on every named table with the grant option and is denied none of them (`may_grant_on`); everything else still needs a superuser, until object owners land.
- **Simplified.** Grants do not yet record their grantor: the ACL's grantor is still the owner, and `REVOKE ... CASCADE` does not follow the grants a role made with its option. Both come with object owners, the next part of this roadmap item.
- **Proof.** Unit tests: a role granting on with its option, refused for a privilege it holds no option for, `has_table_privilege` with `WITH GRANT OPTION`, `REVOKE GRANT OPTION FOR` keeping the privilege and removing the right; the ACL text with stars.
- **Security review.** As a D35 change it needs a security-only review pass once built: who may grant, and that denials win over options.
