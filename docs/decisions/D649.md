## D649 A German stemmer and the german text-search configuration — Decided by the code-only session (2026-11-12, D355); roadmap M8, D30, D195

PostgreSQL ships `to_tsvector('german', ...)`. German applications that moved their search to
PostgreSQL use it, and EggDB answered that the configuration does not exist. D195 wrote only the
English Snowball stemmer.

- `eggdb-search` gets a `german` module written from Snowball's published German algorithm. It
  first reads `ß` as `ss`, and marks `u` and `y` between vowels so they count as consonants. The
  regions R1 (at least three letters in) and R2 follow the same rule as English. Three suffix
  steps follow, each taking the longest suffix in its list and acting only when its conditions
  hold:
  - `em ern er` / `e en es` (then `niss` → `nis`) / `s` after a valid s-ending, in R1;
  - `en er est` / `st` after a valid st-ending with three letters before it, in R1;
  - in R2, `end ung` (then `ig` not after `e`), `ig ik isch` not after `e`, `lich heit` (then
    `er` or `en` in R1), and `keit` (then `lich` or `ig` in R2).

  At the end the marks are lowered and umlauts folded.
- Snowball's German stop word list is dropped from vectors and queries, as PostgreSQL's
  `german` configuration does.
- `Config::German` is named `german` (and `pg_catalog.german`), so `to_tsvector`, the
  `to_tsquery` family and `ts_headline` take it.
- Test: the module's examples (`häuser` → `haus`, `laufen` → `lauf`, `kinder` → `kind`,
  `straße` → `strass`, and `glücklich` and `schönheit` keeping their R2-guarded suffixes).
  Snowball's full German vocabulary file should be checked when the build runs.
