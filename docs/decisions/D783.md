## D783 Syslog over TLS (RFC 5425) and beyond loopback — Decided by the code-only session (2026-11-12, D355); roadmap M16, syslog over TLS and beyond loopback (D315)

- When `eggdb serve` has a certificate (`--tls-certificate` and `--tls-key`, or `--tls-self-signed`), the TCP side of
  `--syslog-listen` speaks syslog over TLS (RFC 5425): each accepted connection completes a TLS handshake with the
  server's certificate (the one the PostgreSQL listener uses, so the same minimum version applies) before its frames
  are read with the octet-counting and line-end framing of D315. A handshake that fails closes the connection and
  counts as one dropped message.
- With TLS, syslog may listen on a non-loopback address, since the transport is then encrypted and the server
  authenticated. UDP cannot carry TLS, so on such an address only TCP is bound; UDP stays on loopback addresses.
  Without TLS the loopback-only rule of D315 holds, and the refusal now says how to get TLS.
- The collector's per-connection reader takes any byte stream, so plain and TLS connections share the framing,
  batching, idle timeout and sender address (D499) code.
- Client certificates (mutual TLS, RFC 5425's recommended authentication of senders), DTLS for datagrams (RFC 6012),
  and rsyslog and syslog-ng in CI are left.
