## D592 LRU eviction for memcached items — Decided by the code-only session (2026-11-12, D355); roadmap M7, D194

memcached is a cache: under `-m` it evicts the least recently used items instead of growing. EggDB's
memcached listener stored every item until it expired or was deleted.

- `eggdb serve --memcached-max-memory <size>` sets the limit. It is off (0) by default, so the
  behaviour without it is unchanged.
- An item counts its key, its stored bytes and a 48-byte header, the size of memcached's own item
  header.
- `eggdb_memcached::Lru` sits on `Shared` and keeps, in memory, each item's size and a tick of its
  last use, plus the order of the ticks.
  - It is built on first use by scanning the items already in the keyspace, so a restart starts
    from the stored items in key order.
  - Every read and write goes through `commands::read` and `commands::write`, which now wrap the
    keys. A hit moves an item to the front; a put places it with its new size; a delete or
    `flush_all` forgets it.
- After a write that leaves the items over the limit, the oldest items are deleted in one more
  write, never the ones that write just stored, until the items fit again. `evictions` counts them.
- `stats` adds `bytes`, `curr_items`, `limit_maxbytes` and `evictions`. `stats settings` reports
  `maxbytes` and `evictions on|off`.
- Expired items still count until a read finds them or a write replaces them, as in memcached
  before its crawler runs.

Not done: memcached's slab classes and per-class LRU, the item crawler, and `lru_crawler`
commands. Eviction works across the whole item space, like memcached with a single slab class.
