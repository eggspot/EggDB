## D977 PostgreSQL's array slices — Decided by the code-only session (2026-11-13, D355); roadmap M5, D19 and D288's arrays

- `a[lo:hi]`, `a[:hi]`, `a[lo:]` and `a[:]` take the elements from subscript `lo` to `hi`, as PostgreSQL's slices of a
  one-dimensional array do: bounds are clamped to the array's own (so `a[0:9]` of a four-element array is the whole
  array, and a range outside it is the empty array), the result's subscripts start at 1, and a `NULL` bound gives
  `NULL`. An omitted bound is the array's lower or upper bound (`array_lower(a, 1)`, `array_upper(a, 1)`), which is
  what PostgreSQL substitutes.
- The parser turns a slice into the internal function `array_slice(array, lo, hi)` beside `array_subscript`, so no new
  expression kind reaches the exhaustive matches; its type is the array's, and its column is named after the array
  column, as PostgreSQL names it.
- Left: multidimensional arrays, whose slices take one range per dimension.
