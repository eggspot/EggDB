## D1213 CONNECT BY over a join — Decided by the code-only session (2026-11-17, D355); roadmap M10, Oracle's hierarchical queries (D304)

- **What users get.** An Oracle query with `START WITH ... CONNECT BY` over `JOIN ... ON` or a comma list of tables walks the joined rows, as Oracle does: the join comes first (its `ON` conditions, and the `WHERE` conditions naming columns of two or more of the joined tables), then the walk, then the other `WHERE` conditions, `GROUP BY`, `HAVING` and `ORDER BY`. `ORDER SIBLINGS BY` takes a column, a select-list alias or a position.
- **How.** D304's rewrite reads a derived table that walks the hierarchy of its source. Over a join, the derived table's own columns would repeat names (two `id` columns) and lose their table qualifiers, so the rewrite passes each column the outer query reads through the walk instead: a new `HierarchyOutput::Current(input)` carries the walked row's value of an inner input (one input per distinct column expression, so the select list, `GROUP BY` and `ORDER BY` share it) to a hidden column of the derived table. The inner query keeps the join and gets the join conditions split off `WHERE`; a condition is a join condition when its qualified columns name two or more of the `FROM` labels, holds no hierarchical operator and is not a subquery.
- **Left.** `SELECT *` or `t.*` over a join (`0A000`), unqualified columns in a comma join's `WHERE` are treated as filters after the walk, correlated subqueries of the outer query reading the joined tables, and Oracle's outer join operator `(+)`.
- **Proof.** `hierarchy_tests.rs`: the same walk over `JOIN ... ON` and over a comma join with the join condition in `WHERE`, a one-table condition applied after the walk, and `GROUP BY` over a joined column; `SELECT *` over a join answers `0A000`.
