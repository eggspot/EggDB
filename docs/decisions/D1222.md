## D1222 Flux's array.from — Decided by the code-only session (2026-11-17, D355); roadmap M16, InfluxQL and Flux (D220, D682, D1064)

- **What users get.** `import "array"` and `array.from(rows: [{...}, ...])` start a Flux pipeline from rows written in the query, as InfluxDB's `array.from` does: one table, ungrouped, whose columns are the records' members, piped on like any stream (`|> filter(...)`, `|> map(...)`, `|> group(...)`) or assigned to a variable and joined.
- **How.** A new statement head beside `from()` and `join`: the parser takes a first call named `array.from` as the head, and `array_rows` turns its list of records into a `Stream` (strings, numbers as floats, booleans, RFC 3339 times; the stream's bounds span the rows' `_time` values, so windows over it work).
- **Left.** Integers kept as integers (the parser keeps every number as a float), durations and nested records in rows, `array.map`, `array.filter` and `array.concat`, and Flux's other packages.
- **Proof.** A unit test in `flux.rs`: two rows with times built, the bounds checked, a filter keeping one, and a row that is not a record refused.
