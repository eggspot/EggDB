## D212 Sharding, online moves and cross-shard transactions in eggdb-cluster, logic first — Decided by the build loop (by judgement, 2026-10-31); refines D4, D18 and D211

Parts 4.M15-2 and 4.M15-3 ask for sharding by hash, range, time or region with online moves,
and SQL across shards with serializable transactions and regions with geo pinning.

- **A shard map with an epoch.** `eggdb-cluster` routes a key to its shard (FNV-1a hash into equal
  ranges, sorted split points, big-endian time buckets, or a region prefix) and the shard to its
  owner. Every ownership change raises the map's epoch and a node serves only requests routed
  with the current epoch, so no write lands on a shard's former home after a move.
- **Moves are online**: the new owner copies a snapshot while the old one serves, then applies
  the writes made meanwhile, and the map cuts over only when nothing is left to apply. The
  rebalancer plans the fewest moves that leave nodes within one shard of each other, first
  re-homing shards of departed nodes, and never moves a region-pinned shard out of its region
  (geo pinning).
- **Cross-shard transactions use two-phase commit with strict two-phase locking**: every key a
  transaction reads or writes stays locked until the decision, which makes them serializable;
  a held lock makes the newcomer abort and retry, so there are no deadlocks; the coordinator
  records the decision before sending it and an undecided transaction is aborted on recovery.
  A seeded simulation of transfers with conflicts and coordinator crashes checks that money is
  never created or lost and nothing stays in doubt.
- **The map and the coordinator's record live in the Raft log** (D211) once the cluster runs;
  this slice is the logic, tested in isolation.

Left: the cluster process (`eggdb serve --cluster`), replicated map and decision log on Raft, the
data copy itself over the replication protocol (D209), a distributed SQL planner pushing work to
shards, cross-region latency-aware placement, timestamps for snapshot reads across shards, and
simulation of rebalancing under load.
