## D1232 Per-role connection limits — Decided by the code-only session (2026-11-17, D355); roadmap M6c, quotas and admission control (D25, D234, D1204)

- **What users get.** PostgreSQL's `CREATE ROLE ... CONNECTION LIMIT n` and `ALTER ROLE ... CONNECTION LIMIT n` (`-1` lifts it) and MySQL's `MAX_USER_CONNECTIONS n` and `MAX_CONNECTIONS_PER_HOUR n` (0 lifts them) are stored and enforced when a role logs in on any protocol: past its open sessions the login is refused with `53300` (`too many connections for role "x"`), past its logins in the hour with `53400`, and in MySQL sessions with error 1226 naming the resource, as MySQL does. A session's place is freed when it closes.
- **How.** The two limits join D1204's in the role's limit record (`ROLE_LIMIT_SPACE`), which grows from 8 to 16 bytes; 8-byte records written before read as having no connection limits. `admission::connect` counts open sessions and the hour's logins per database and role in the process; a login under an open-session limit gets an `OpenConnection` kept in the session's settings, whose drop frees the place. Roles without connection limits take no lock and no place.
- **Left.** Concurrency limits (statements running at once per role), counts shared by a cluster's nodes, `pg_roles.rolconnlimit` showing the limit, and the database-level `CONNECTION LIMIT` of `CREATE DATABASE`.
- **Proof.** A unit test of `connect` (two places, a third refused, freed on drop, the hour's logins, MySQL's 1226) and an end-to-end test: a `CONNECTION LIMIT 1` role's second session refused until the first closes, `-1` lifting it, and MySQL's two options stored.
