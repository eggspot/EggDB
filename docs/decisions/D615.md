## D615 AMQP priority queues — Decided by the code-only session (2026-11-12, D355); roadmap M16, D214, D516

Celery, Sidekiq-on-RabbitMQ and job systems declare `x-max-priority` and publish urgent work with
a higher `priority`. EggDB refused such queues with `NOT_IMPLEMENTED`.

- `x-max-priority` (1 to 255; larger values are capped at 255) is kept in the queue's policy.
  The refusal is gone.
- A message's priority is its `priority` basic property, read past the earlier properties
  (`field_table::message_priority`), capped at the queue's maximum. A message without one has
  priority 0.
- Messages are stored in one queue in arrival order, as before. Priority is applied when they are
  handed out:
  - `basic.get` leases up to the first 1,000 ready messages and takes the highest-priority one
    (the oldest among equals). It gives the others back unleased, so their receive counts and
    redelivered flags stay as they were.
  - Consumers get each leased batch (up to their credit) sorted by priority, highest first,
    oldest first among equals.
- This is close to RabbitMQ but not the same:
  - RabbitMQ keeps a sub-queue per priority, so a high-priority message overtakes every waiting
    lower one.
  - Here it overtakes within the 1,000-message window of `basic.get`, or within one consumer
    batch. With a prefetch of 1, a consumer sees arrival order.
- A unit test reads the priority past the content type and delivery mode.

Not done: per-priority storage, so that priority holds across a backlog larger than the window
and for prefetch-1 consumers.
