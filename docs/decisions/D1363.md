## D1363 EXPLAIN ANALYZE measures the scan inside a subplan — Decided by the code-only session (2026-11-25, D355); roadmap M6c "Times on each iterator inside a subplan in EXPLAIN ANALYZE" (D327)

- **What users get.** Under a correlated `SubPlan`, the scan that reads the subquery's table now carries its own `(actual time=... rows=... loops=...)`, as PostgreSQL prints it: `loops` is how many times the subplan ran and `rows` the rows the scan read per loop, so `->  Limit (actual ... rows=1 loops=3)` stands over `->  Seq Scan on onek o (actual ... rows=250 loops=3)`. Before, only the subplan's top line was measured and the scan under it showed nothing.
- **How.** D327's step times let one select meter run at a time, and a subplan's select never took it, so the statement's own select keeps its figures. A running subplan now opens a slot of its own: the first select claimed inside it gets a meter whose figures go to that subplan's scan record (`MeasuredSubplan` with `scan: true`), summed over loops, and its drop leaves the statement's meter alone. A subplan inside a subplan opens its own slot and gives the outer one back when it ends. The scan's time per loop is from the subplan's start to the scan's last row.
- **Where it shows.** On the leaf line of a subplan over one table (not an `Append`), under any `Sort` or `Limit`; a subplan whose plan is the scan alone keeps showing the subplan's own figures there, as before.
- **Left.** The `Sort` between a subplan's `Limit` and its scan, joins inside a subplan, and the same figures in the other plan formats.
- **Proof.** A unit test runs a subplan twice with a scan of 4 and 6 rows and finds the subplan's 2 loops and 5 rows beside its scan's 2 loops and 10 rows, with the statement's own select still metered once; a session test reads `loops=3` on the scan inside a correlated subquery with `ORDER BY ... LIMIT 1`.
