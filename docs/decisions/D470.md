## D470 Fuzzy and phonetic matching: `fuzzystrmatch` and `pg_trgm` functions — Decided by the code-only session (2026-11-11, D355); roadmap M8, D30

- Built in, without `CREATE EXTENSION`, as PostgreSQL's extensions name them:
  `levenshtein(a, b [, insert, delete, substitute])` and `levenshtein_less_equal(..., most)` (the
  distance, or `most + 1` past it; at most 255 characters, as fuzzystrmatch), `soundex`,
  `difference` (0–4 matching Soundex characters), and `metaphone(text, length)` (Lawrence
  Philips' original Metaphone as fuzzystrmatch writes it, `0` for "th", length 1–255).
- `pg_trgm`'s `show_trgm` (each alphanumeric word lowercased and padded with two spaces before and
  one after, its three-character windows as a sorted, distinct `text[]`), `similarity` (shared
  trigrams over all trigrams), `similarity_dist` (1 − similarity), `word_similarity` (the best
  similarity of the first text's trigrams with any run of the second text's trigrams in order)
  and `strict_word_similarity` (runs of whole words only). Inputs are capped at 65,536 characters.
- SQL Server's `SOUNDEX` and `DIFFERENCE` give the same values through the same names.
- Not written: `dmetaphone`, `daitch_mokotoff`, the trigram operators (`%`, `<%`, `<->`, ...) with
  `pg_trgm.similarity_threshold`, trigram indexes, MySQL's longer `SOUNDEX` and `SOUNDS LIKE`,
  Oracle's `UTL_MATCH`, and synonym and stop-word dictionaries.
