## D660 DBMS_CRYPTO hashes and MACs, and DBMS_UTILITY.GET_TIME — Decided by the code-only session (2026-11-12, D355); roadmap M10, D659

Applications hash passwords and fingerprints with `DBMS_CRYPTO.HASH(..., DBMS_CRYPTO.HASH_SH256)`,
sign tokens with `DBMS_CRYPTO.MAC`, and time steps with `DBMS_UTILITY.GET_TIME`. EggDB's own crypto
crate already has the digests and HMAC.

- `DBMS_CRYPTO.HASH(src, typ)` takes `HASH_MD5` (2), `HASH_SH1` (3), `HASH_SH256` (4),
  `HASH_SH384` (5) and `HASH_SH512` (6). `MAC(src, typ, key)` takes `HMAC_SH1` (2) through
  `HMAC_SH512` (5). Both return `RAW`. A `RAW` source is hashed as its bytes and a text (`CLOB`,
  `VARCHAR2`) as its UTF-8 bytes. MD4 and HMAC-MD5 are ORA-28827, since the crypto crate has
  neither.
- `RANDOMBYTES(n)` returns `n` bytes from the secure random source (1 to 32,767; otherwise
  ORA-28234). The encryption procedures (`ENCRYPT`/`DECRYPT` with their cipher-suite constants)
  are left.
- The package constants (`DBMS_CRYPTO.HASH_SH256` and the rest) are read as integer literals
  where they appear without parentheses.
- `DBMS_UTILITY.GET_TIME` (with or without parentheses) is hundredths of a second, wrapped at
  2^31 as Oracle's counter is. Only differences between two readings mean anything.
- Test: SHA-256 of `abc` against its published digest, the constant, and the MD4 refusal.
