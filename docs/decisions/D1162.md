## D1162 QUIC stage 3: a server connection without I/O, with RFC 9002's loss detection and NewReno — Decided by the code-only session (2026-11-14, D355); roadmap M6a (D34, D1160, D1161)

- **The connection.** `eggdb_quic::connection::ServerConnection` is one QUIC server connection, driven without
  I/O. Datagrams go in through `receive(datagram, now)` and out through `poll_transmit(now)`; timers run through
  `timeout()` and `on_timeout(now)`. The UDP server that feeds it is stage 4.
- **Accepting.** `accept` takes the client's first datagram, which must be at least 1200 bytes and start with an
  Initial packet whose destination ID is at least 8 bytes. It derives the Initial keys from that ID, picks the
  caller's random 8-byte connection ID, and sends transport parameters:
  - the original destination ID;
  - a 30 s idle timeout;
  - 16 MiB for the connection and 1 MiB per stream;
  - 100 bidirectional streams;
  - 25 ms max ACK delay;
  - migration disabled.
- **Receiving.** Coalesced packets are split by their Length fields. Each is unprotected with its level's keys and
  dropped silently when it does not verify or repeats a packet number. Received numbers are merged into at most
  64 ranges for ACK frames. A Handshake packet from the client discards the Initial keys, and handshake
  completion discards the Handshake keys (RFC 9001 section 4.9). CRYPTO data is reassembled in order (at most
  64 KiB ahead) and fed to `QuicServerHandshake` (D1161); its events queue CRYPTO data, install packet keys,
  and on completion check the client's transport parameters, including that its `initial_source_connection_id`
  matches. Frames only a server sends, or stream frames before 1-RTT, close the connection with
  `PROTOCOL_VIOLATION`.
- **Streams.** Client-opened bidirectional streams are served, opening every lower-numbered one with them, as
  RFC 9000 section 3.2 says.
  - Data is reassembled per stream; final sizes and stream and connection flow control limits are enforced.
  - `read_stream` returns ordered data and the end, and sends MAX_STREAM_DATA or MAX_DATA once half a window is
    read.
  - `write_stream` queues data, sent within the client's limits.
  - RESET_STREAM and STOP_SENDING are honoured, and PATH_CHALLENGE is answered.
- **Sending.** Each datagram holds at most 1200 bytes of Initial, Handshake and 1-RTT packets, coalesced in
  that order. Each packet carries, in order:
  1. an ACK of its space when one is due;
  2. retransmitted frames;
  3. CRYPTO data split to fit;
  4. at 1-RTT, HANDSHAKE_DONE and stream data.

  A datagram with an ack-eliciting Initial is padded to 1200 bytes, and payloads are padded so header protection
  can sample. Packet numbers use appendix A.2's length. A close sends one CONNECTION_CLOSE: 0x1c with a transport
  code, `0x100` plus the TLS alert for handshake failures, or 0x1d for `close_with` by the application.
- **Recovery (RFC 9002).** `recovery::RttEstimate` keeps the latest, minimum, smoothed and variation RTT. ACK delay
  is subtracted only above the minimum and capped by max ACK delay.
  - A packet is lost three packets or 9/8 RTT behind the largest acknowledged; its retransmittable frames
    (everything but ACK, PADDING, PING and CONNECTION_CLOSE) are queued again.
  - The probe timeout (smoothed + 4 variations + max ACK delay, doubled per probe) resends the unacknowledged
    frames of every space with keys, with a PING when nothing else is ack-eliciting.
  - `recovery::CongestionControl` is NewReno: a 10-datagram start, slow start, congestion avoidance, and one
    halving per loss episode, never below two datagrams. Ack-eliciting packets wait for room in the window;
    ACKs never do.
  - The idle timeout closes the connection silently.
- **Tests.** RTT smoothing with ACK delay, NewReno's growth and single halving, received ranges merging with
  duplicates refused, and a short first datagram refused. An end-to-end exchange with a real QUIC client
  arrives with the stage 4 server, in CI.
