## D666 OTLP export of the server's logs and slow statements — Decided by the code-only session (2026-11-12, D355); roadmap M6c, D25, D188, D567

D567 pushed the server's metrics to an OpenTelemetry collector and left logs and traces. Teams
that send everything through one collector want the server's log lines next to their
applications' logs, and slow statements in their trace views.

- `serve_otlp::HeldTelemetry` keeps the log lines and slow statements waiting for the next
  export, at most 10,000 of each (the oldest dropped first). It holds them only when
  `--otlp-endpoint` is given.
- **Logs.** Every line the server prints, slow-statement lines included, is also held with its
  time. Each export posts them to `/v1/logs` as `INFO` log records whose body is the line, under
  the same `service.name` `eggdb` resource as the metrics.
- **Traces.** With `--log-slow-statements`, each slow statement is also held as a span. It runs
  from its end time minus its duration to its end, and is named `<protocol> statement`. It
  carries OpenTelemetry's database attributes: `db.system` `eggdb`, `db.name`, `db.user`,
  `network.protocol.name`, `db.statement` (the normalized text, literals already replaced) and
  `db.response.returned_rows`. Each span is a server span of its own trace, since clients' trace
  context is not read from the protocols yet. Each export posts them to `/v1/traces`.
- `export_metrics` becomes `export_telemetry`: once a minute it posts the metrics, then the logs
  and spans that are held, if any, and it does a last export when the server stops.
- Protobuf bodies and TLS stay left, as in D567.
- Test: `log_lines_and_slow_statements_become_records_and_spans`, which checks that the two
  requests are valid JSON with the right times and names.
