## D429 pg_cron's `'n seconds'` and `$` schedules, `cron.alter_job`, and jobs run as their user — Decided by the code-only session (2026-11-11, D355); roadmap M10, extends D200

- **Schedules.** `CronSchedule::parse` reads pg_cron's two additions: `$` in the day-of-month field
  (alone or in a list, `1,$`) is the month's last day, which `Minute` now knows
  (`last_day_of_month`); and `'n seconds'` (or `'n second'`, n from 1 to 59) runs every n
  seconds, counted from the start of each minute, as pg_cron's interval jobs do on a minute's
  boundary.
- **Running.** The minute runner (`run_due_jobs`) skips second schedules; a new
  `run_due_second_jobs`, which `eggdb serve`'s job thread calls once a second, runs only them. A
  second job runs at most once a second and, like the rest, in its own transaction, recorded in
  `cron.job_run_details`.
- **As its user.** A job now runs after `SET SESSION AUTHORIZATION "username"` with `cron.job`'s
  `username`, so a job has its scheduler's rights, as pg_cron runs it; a job whose user no longer
  exists fails and records the error. Jobs scheduled before this keep the user that `cron.job`'s
  default gave them.
- **`cron.alter_job(job_id, schedule, command, database, username, active)`**, as pg_cron has it:
  the arguments given (not `NULL`) replace the job's, and an unknown job raises `Job n does not
  exist`. It is part of `CREATE EXTENSION pg_cron`'s script, so installations made earlier don't
  have it until the extension is dropped and created again: a known gap, as EggDB has no `ALTER
  EXTENSION ... UPDATE` yet.
