## D467 Elasticsearch scroll — Decided by the code-only session (2026-11-11, D355); roadmap M8, D196 and D325

- `_search?scroll=<keep-alive>` (and `/<index>/_search?scroll=`) runs the search once, keeps every
  ranked hit (no `from + size` window; `size` alone must stay within 10,000) and answers the first
  `size` hits with a `_scroll_id`. `_search/scroll` (GET or POST, the id in the body's
  `scroll_id`, the query or the path) answers the next `size` hits and renews the keep-alive
  (the request's `scroll`, else the last one); an exhausted scroll answers empty hits until it is
  cleared or expires. `DELETE _search/scroll` frees the ids named in the body or path, or `_all`,
  answering `{"succeeded":true,"num_freed":n}` (404 when none of the named ids existed).
- Keep-alives take Elasticsearch's time units (`ms`, `s`, `m`, `h`, `d`, `micros`, `nanos`) up to
  24 hours. At most 500 contexts are open (Elasticsearch's default); a new one past that answers
  429 `es_rejected_execution_exception`. Expired contexts are dropped on every scroll call.
- A context holds the search's page as built for the stream (D463): the searched documents and
  the ranked hit list, each hit rendered only when its page is sent. Contexts live in the API's
  memory, not the file, so a server restart ends them, as a node restart does in Elasticsearch.
- Scroll pages are whole responses, not streams: a page is at most `size` hits.
