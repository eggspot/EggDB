## D804 SHA-3 and ML-KEM-768 in eggdb-crypto — Decided by the code-only session (2026-11-12, D355); roadmap M6b, post-quantum key exchange

- `eggdb-crypto` gains Keccak-f[1600] and the FIPS 202 functions EggDB needs: `sha3_256`, `sha3_512` and `shake256`
  over several input slices, and a crate-internal SHAKE128/SHAKE256 sponge that squeezes any amount, written from FIPS
  202 with its round constants and rotation offsets, and checked against FIPS 202's empty-message and `abc` digests.
- ML-KEM-768 (FIPS 203, the final standard of August 2024): `mlkem768_key_pair(seed)` from the 64 random bytes `d ‖ z`,
  `mlkem768_encapsulate(ek, m)` with 32 random bytes and `mlkem768_decapsulate(dk, c)`, with FIPS 203's parameters
  (k = 3, η1 = η2 = 2, du = 10, dv = 4), its domain-separated `G(d ‖ k)`, the NTT with ζ = 17 and the bit-reversed
  zeta table computed at compile time, SampleNTT by rejection from SHAKE128, centred binomial noise from SHAKE256, and
  the input checks: the encapsulation key's modulus check, the decapsulation key's hash check, and implicit rejection
  (a ciphertext that does not re-encrypt to itself gives `J(z ‖ c)`, compared in constant time). Arithmetic is plain
  `% q` on small integers rather than Montgomery or Barrett reduction: slower, but every step is the specification's.
- The randomness comes from the caller, as for X25519 and P-256 in EggDB's TLS, so tests are deterministic and the TLS
  layer keeps using the platform's secure random source.
- Left: the X25519MLKEM768 hybrid group in TLS 1.3 (the next batch), NIST's ML-KEM known-answer vectors in the tests,
  and a constant-time review of the modulo operations.
