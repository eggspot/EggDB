## D1026 Failure detection, fencing and planned switchover — Decided by the code-only session (2026-11-13, D355); roadmap M14 (D18, D209, D1024); data-critical (D35)

- **Fencing.** `Database::fence` refuses every new write transaction with a reason, while batches a replica applies
  still go through. A replica runs fenced from its start (clients get "this server is a replica of ..."), a primary
  handing over is fenced, and a primary that lost its majority fences itself.
- **The group.** `--replication-peers` lists the other servers' replication listeners; every server of the group
  runs one (a replica's answers status, promotion and follow requests from its peers, signed with the same HMAC
  proof as replication itself, D1024). `--replication-advertise` is the address peers reach this server at.
- **Automatic failover.** With `--failover-after <seconds>`, a replica that heard nothing from its primary for that
  long asks its peers for their status. It promotes itself only when a majority of the whole group (the peers, itself
  and the lost primary) answered, no peer still reached the primary within the delay or reports being primary, and
  `eggdb-replication`'s election (newest term, then most applied) picks it. It starts the next term above every term
  it saw (`Database::promote`, which resumes the history so the first batch names the old term as the one before
  it), and tells the peers that answered to follow it. A peer told to follow switches its follower at once.
- **The primary's lease.** A primary with peers and a failover delay counts the replicas connected to it; when it has
  not had a majority of the group for longer than the delay it fences itself, so the side of a partition without a
  majority never takes writes while the other side promotes a replica. It takes the fence away when the majority is
  back and no peer reports a newer term; if one does, it stays fenced, to be copied again as a replica.
- **Planned switchover.** `SWITCHOVER TO 'host:port'`, run by a superuser on the primary, fences the primary, waits
  (up to 30 seconds) until that peer applied the primary's last batch, promotes it in the next term, retires the old
  primary's capture and makes it follow the new primary, and tells the other peers to follow it too; nothing
  committed is lost, and the old primary becomes a replica without being copied again. The statement reaches the
  server through a hook `eggdb_sql::set_switchover_hook`, which only a replicating server installs.
- Left: cascading replicas, a witness that holds no data for two-server groups, and Jepsen-style checks against real
  processes (the next M14 item).
