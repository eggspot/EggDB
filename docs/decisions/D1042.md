## D1042 Snapshot reads across shards by timestamp, with serializable distributed transactions — Decided by the code-only session (2026-11-13, D355); roadmap M15 (D18, D212)

- **Timestamps.** `eggdb_cluster::clock` adds hybrid logical clocks: a `Timestamp` is milliseconds since the epoch in
  the high 48 bits and a counter in the low 16, so timestamps stay near wall time and still order every event a node
  sees. `HybridClock::now` gives a timestamp after every one it gave or saw; `observe` takes in another node's, and
  refuses one further ahead than the cluster's maximum clock offset (`ClockSkew`), as the HLC paper (Kulkarni et al.)
  and the systems built on it do.
- **Versions.** A `Participant` now keeps every committed write as a version stamped with its commit timestamp,
  instead of the latest value only. `prepare_at` prepares at a timestamp from the shard's clock; the coordinator
  commits at `commit_timestamp`, the largest prepare timestamp of the participants, and `decide_at` applies the
  versions there. The old `prepare` and `decide` remain and stamp after the shard's last commit.
- **Snapshot reads.** `read_at(key, at)` returns the newest version at or before `at`. A transaction prepared at or
  before `at` that writes the key may still commit at or before `at`, so the read reports `Wait` with its id rather
  than guess. The read also raises the shard's read floor to `at`, so anything that prepares there afterwards does so
  later and the snapshot never changes once read. `snapshot_read` reads keys of several shards at one timestamp: a
  distributed transaction is then seen on every shard or on none, because its versions carry one commit timestamp
  and the wait rule covers the shards where it is still prepared.
- **Serializable.** Read-write transactions keep D212's strict two-phase locking; their commit timestamps follow the
  lock order, because a transaction commits after its prepares and every reader it conflicted with released its locks
  before that. Read-only transactions read a snapshot at a timestamp and take no locks, which is serializable at that
  point in the order.
- `collect_garbage(oldest)` drops versions no snapshot at or after `oldest` can read.
- Tested by a seeded simulation of transfers across three shards with clocks advancing unevenly, decisions delayed and
  snapshot reads at past timestamps: every snapshot sees either none of the accounts or all of them with the total
  unchanged, and reading every snapshot again at the end gives the same values.
- Left: carrying timestamps through the SQL path of sharded tables (D1041) and through `serve --cluster`, where
  the database's own commit stamps would serve as the shard clock.
- Learned from the HLC paper and the descriptions of Spanner and CockroachDB's transaction models; no code was read.
