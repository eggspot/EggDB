## D609 S2 cell ids — Decided by the code-only session (2026-11-12, D355); roadmap M12, D57, D250

S2 cells are how BigQuery, Google's own systems and many mobility pipelines bucket points:
`S2_CELLIDFROMPOINT(point, level)` gives a 64-bit id whose prefix is its parent cell, so cells
join, group and range-scan as plain integers.

- `eggdb-geo`'s new `s2::s2_cell_id(latitude, longitude, level)` follows the S2 geometry library's
  published numbering:
  1. The point goes onto the unit sphere and then onto the cube face of its largest axis.
  2. It gets the face's `(u, v)` and the quadratic `uv → st` transform.
  3. That gives leaf coordinates `(i, j)` on a 2³⁰ grid.
  4. The Hilbert curve position comes from the 4-bit lookup table, built from the four
     orientations' quadrant orders.
  5. The id is `face << 61 | position << 1 | 1`, truncated to the level by clearing the low bits
     and setting the level's lowest bit.
- `s2_token` writes an id as hexadecimal without trailing zeros, and `X` for 0.
- SQL (PostgreSQL dialect, with the other spatial functions):
  - `S2_CellIdFromPoint(point [, level])` takes a point's x as longitude and y as latitude,
    defaults to level 30 (BigQuery's default), and returns a `bigint`, with ids of faces 4 and 5
    wrapping negative as BigQuery's `INT64` does.
  - `S2_CellIdToToken(id)` returns the token.
  - A level outside 0 to 30, or a geometry that is not a point, is refused.
- Tests:
  - the point (0, 0) is leaf id `0x1000000000000001` and face-0 cell `0x1000000000000000` (token
    `1`), which follows from the Hilbert curve's halfway position on face 0;
  - points on each axis land on faces 1 to 5;
  - every parent level of a Paris point has its lowest bit at `2·(30 − level)` and holds the leaf.

Not done: H3 (its icosahedral grid is a larger piece of work), S2 coverings of regions,
`S2_CellIdToParent` and the other cell functions.
