## D1310 Sequence privileges — Decided by the code-only session (2026-11-25, D355); roadmap M8 roles (D183)

- **What users get.** `GRANT` and `REVOKE` `USAGE`, `SELECT`, `UPDATE` (or `ALL`) `ON SEQUENCE s` to roles, with `WITH GRANT OPTION` and `REVOKE GRANT OPTION FOR`, and a role that is not a superuser may then call the sequence functions only as PostgreSQL allows: `nextval` with `USAGE` or `UPDATE`, `currval` and `lastval` with `USAGE` or `SELECT`, and `setval` with `UPDATE`; otherwise `42501 permission denied for sequence s`. The owner of the table a sequence belongs to (`OWNED BY`, as a `serial` column's sequence does) holds all of them, so a role inserting into its own table keeps drawing ids. Privileges held through a role and through `PUBLIC` count. A grant on a missing sequence fails with `42P01`, a privilege a sequence does not take with `0LP01`, and dropping a sequence forgets its grants. Superuser sessions (embedded use and the server's own user) are never checked, and identity columns' internal sequences are not either, as in PostgreSQL.
- **How.** Sequence grants are a sixth kind of object grant (D1190) in the same key space; the privilege bits gained `SELECT` and `UPDATE`. When a statement of a checked role loads its sequences, it also works out each sequence's usable bits for the role once, and the sequence functions check them.
- **Left.** `GRANT ... ON ALL SEQUENCES IN SCHEMA` and default privileges, sequences in `pg_class.relacl` and `information_schema.usage_privileges`.
- **Proof.** A roles test draws from a granted sequence, refuses `setval` without `UPDATE` and a sequence never granted, grants `UPDATE` and sets it, revokes `USAGE`, refuses `EXECUTE` and a missing sequence, and starts a re-created sequence with no grants.
