## D1332 PostgreSQL's multiranges — Decided by the code-only session (2026-11-25, D355); roadmap "PostgreSQL's network address, geometric, bit string, `money`, `xml` and multirange types" (D19)

- **What users get.** The six built-in multirange types of PostgreSQL 14 and later (`int4multirange`, `int8multirange`, `nummultirange`, `datemultirange`, `tsmultirange`, `tstzmultirange`) as columns, casts and constants: `'{[1,5), [10,20)}'`, the constructors named after each type, `multirange(range)` and casts from a range. Values keep their ranges sorted, merged when they overlap or touch, and empty ranges dropped, and print as PostgreSQL prints them. Operators: `+` (union), `-` (difference), `*` (intersection), `@>` and `<@` against elements, ranges and multiranges, `&&` against ranges and multiranges. Functions: `lower`, `upper`, `isempty`, `lower_inc`, `upper_inc`, `lower_inf`, `upper_inf`, `range_merge`; aggregates `range_agg` (ranges or multiranges into a multirange) and `range_intersect_agg`. OIDs 4451 and 4532–4536 in text and binary form (a count, then each range with its length), with `pg_type` rows (`typtype` `m`) and their arrays.
- **How.** A child module of `ranges`, `ranges::multiranges`, reuses the range bound comparison, `make`, union and overlap helpers, so a multirange's ranges are canonical exactly as a range's are. A `DefinedValue::Multirange` keeps the multirange type OID and its ranges; stored rows tag it 49 with the OID, the count and each range in the range encoding. `range_agg` and `range_intersect_agg` are new aggregate functions collecting their inputs as `array_agg` does.
- **Left.** Multiranges of user-defined range types (`CREATE TYPE ... AS RANGE` gets no multirange type yet), `unnest(multirange)`, the position operators `<<`, `>>`, `&<`, `&>` and `-|-` on multiranges, and B-tree indexes ordered by multiranges in keys (keys hold their text).
- **Proof.** Unit tests for reading, merging, printing and every operator; a session test stores two multiranges, filters by containment, aggregates ranges with `range_agg`, intersects two columns and calls `upper` and `range_merge`.

**Status:** code written, unbuilt (code-only mode, D355).
