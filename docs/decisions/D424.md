## D424 MariaDB's sequences in the MySQL dialect — Decided by the code-only session (2026-11-11, D355); roadmap M10

- The MySQL dialect takes MariaDB's sequence statements on EggDB's one sequence engine (the one
  PostgreSQL's, Oracle's and SQL Server's sequences share): `CREATE SEQUENCE [IF NOT EXISTS]`,
  `ALTER SEQUENCE ... RESTART [WITH | =] n` and the other options, and `DROP SEQUENCE [IF
  EXISTS]`. MariaDB's spellings are read: `=` after `INCREMENT [BY]`, `MINVALUE`, `MAXVALUE`,
  `START [WITH]` and `CACHE`; `NOMINVALUE`, `NOMAXVALUE`, `NOCACHE` and `NOCYCLE`; and a trailing
  `ENGINE=` or `COMMENT=`, which are accepted and dropped.
- Values: `NEXTVAL(s)` and `NEXT VALUE FOR s` take the next value as Oracle's `s.NEXTVAL` does;
  `LASTVAL(s)` and `PREVIOUS VALUE FOR s` read the session's last value as `s.CURRVAL` does, which
  fails before the session's first `NEXTVAL` where MariaDB gives `NULL`: a known difference.
  `SETVAL(s, n [, is_used [, 0]])` is PostgreSQL's `setval` (`is_used` as its `is_called`); a round
  other than 0 is refused.
- Left: `SELECT * FROM s` on a sequence (MariaDB shows it as a one-row table), `CREATE OR REPLACE
  SEQUENCE` and `SHOW CREATE SEQUENCE`.
