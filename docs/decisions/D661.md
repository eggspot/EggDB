## D661 AES decryption, and DBMS_CRYPTO.ENCRYPT and DECRYPT — Decided by the code-only session (2026-11-12, D355); roadmap M10, D660, D23

`DBMS_CRYPTO.ENCRYPT(src, DBMS_CRYPTO.ENCRYPT_AES256 + DBMS_CRYPTO.CHAIN_CBC +
DBMS_CRYPTO.PAD_PKCS5, key, iv)` is how Oracle applications encrypt a column themselves. D660
left it out because EggDB's AES encrypted only: TLS's modes never decrypt a block.

- `eggdb-crypto`'s `Aes` gets `decrypt_block`, FIPS 197's inverse cipher. The inverse S-box is
  computed like the forward one: the inverse affine map on bit planes, then the GF(2^8) inverse,
  with no table and no data-dependent branch. Inverse mix columns uses the usual
  pre-multiplication before the forward mix.
- `DBMS_CRYPTO.ENCRYPT`/`DECRYPT(src, typ, key[, iv])` read `typ` as Oracle's sum of three parts:
  - a cipher: `ENCRYPT_AES` (key length chooses), `ENCRYPT_AES128`/`192`/`256`;
  - a chaining: `CHAIN_CBC` (the default) or `CHAIN_ECB`;
  - a padding: `PAD_PKCS5` (the default), `PAD_ZERO` or `PAD_NONE`.

  The IV defaults to sixteen zero bytes, as Oracle's does. DES, 3DES, RC4, CFB and OFB are
  ORA-28827. A key of the wrong length is ORA-28234. A bad length, padding or IV is ORA-28817.
- The cipher and chaining constants and the `AES_CBC_PKCS5`-style shorthands join D660's constants
  as integer literals.
- Tests: FIPS 197 Appendix C.1 encrypted and decrypted, the inverse S-box inverting the S-box,
  AES-256 round trips in CBC/PKCS5, ECB/PKCS5 and CBC/zero padding, and the DES refusal.
