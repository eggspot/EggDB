## D526 MQTT persistent sessions and QoS 1 and 2 to subscribers — Decided by the code-only session (2026-11-11, D355); roadmap M16

- **QoS:** subscriptions are now granted the QoS asked for, up to 2 (they were capped at 1).
  Every QoS 1 and 2 message sent to a subscriber is kept in that subscriber's in-flight list
  under its packet id until `PUBACK` (QoS 1) or `PUBREC` then `PUBCOMP` (QoS 2) settles it;
  packet ids skip ids still in flight, and a subscriber holds at most 1,000 messages in flight
  (more are not sent to it).
- **Persistent sessions:** a 3.1.1 client connecting with Clean Session 0, or a 5.0 client with
  a Session Expiry Interval above 0, keeps its session when it goes away: its subscriptions, its
  messages in flight (and which QoS 2 ones were released), and the QoS 1 and 2 messages that
  arrive for its subscriptions meanwhile (at most 1,000, the oldest dropped first). When it
  connects again with the same client id and without a clean start, `CONNACK` says the session
  is present, unreleased messages in flight are sent again with the DUP flag, released ones get
  their `PUBREL` again, and the queued messages follow. A clean start discards the session. A
  3.1.1 client asking to keep a session with an empty client id is refused (0x02).
- **Bounds:** sessions are kept in memory, at most 10,000 of them and for at most 7 days (the
  5.0 expiry interval when shorter), so they do not survive a restart of the server; a second
  connection with a client id that is connected ends the first one's session, as MQTT says.
- **Left out:** keeping sessions in the file, shared subscriptions in kept sessions (they are
  delivered live only), Receive Maximum and flow control, and server-assigned client ids.

**Status:** code written, unbuilt (code-only mode, D355).
