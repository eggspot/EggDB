## D1067 The MATCH_RECOGNIZE clause and MEASURES — Decided by the code-only session (2026-11-13, D355); roadmap M16 (D55, D768)

- `FROM <table or subquery> MATCH_RECOGNIZE (...) [AS] alias` is parsed in every dialect
  (`crates/eggdb-sql/src/parser/match_recognize.rs`). It is rewritten into a derived table, the way
  `TABLESAMPLE` is, built from:
  - `mr_numbered`: the source's rows, each numbered within its partition in `ORDER BY` order, with one
    flag per `DEFINE` variable. A condition's `PREV(x[, n])` and `NEXT(x[, n])` become `lag` and `lead` over
    the partition, and its own `VAR.` qualifier is dropped.
  - `mr_matches`: per partition, `row_pattern_matches` (D768) over the flags gives each match's first row,
    last row and classifiers, numbered as `MATCH_NUMBER()`.
  - the output: one row per match, with the `PARTITION BY` columns and then the `MEASURES`.
- **`MEASURES`** use final semantics (`FINAL` is accepted; `RUNNING` is refused as not supported):
  - `FIRST(expr)` and `LAST(expr)`, over the match or one variable's rows (`LAST(DOWN.price)`);
  - a bare column or `VAR.column`, which is its last row's value;
  - `COUNT(*)`, `COUNT(VAR.*)` and `COUNT`, `SUM`, `AVG`, `MIN` and `MAX` of an expression, over the match
    or one variable's rows;
  - `CLASSIFIER()` (the last row's) and `MATCH_NUMBER()`.
  - Each becomes a scalar subquery on `mr_numbered`, so values keep their column types.
  - An expression may name one pattern variable. Pattern variables come from `DEFINE` and from the pattern
    itself, so undefined ones such as `STRT` can be measured too.
- Accepted: `ONE ROW PER MATCH` and `AFTER MATCH SKIP PAST LAST ROW` (the matcher's mode). Refused as not
  supported: `ALL ROWS PER MATCH`, the other skip modes and `SUBSET`.
- `PARTITION BY` takes column names, since the output names them.
- The lexer now reads `{` and `}` as operator characters, so `{n,m}` quantifiers reach the parser. Outside
  a pattern they are still a syntax error.
- The clause scanner tracks parentheses and never indexes past the tokens: a malformed clause is a syntax
  error, never a panic (D9).
- Learned from: SQL:2016 (ISO/IEC 19075-5, row pattern recognition) as public documentation describes it.
