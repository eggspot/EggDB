## D1313 ALTER ROLE ... SET, and concurrency limits per role — Decided by the code-only session (2026-11-25, D355); roadmap M12 quotas and admission control (D25, D234)

- **What users get.** PostgreSQL's per-role settings: `ALTER ROLE r SET name {TO | =} value`, `ALTER ROLE r IN DATABASE d SET ...`, `ALTER ROLE ALL [IN DATABASE d] SET ...`, `SET name TO DEFAULT`, `RESET name` and `RESET ALL` (also as `ALTER USER`). A session that logs in as the role starts with them, the most specific winning as in PostgreSQL: every role on the database, then the role everywhere, then the role on the database; a setting the session cannot take is skipped, as PostgreSQL only warns. Dropping a role forgets its settings. With `eggdb.workload_queue` among them, every session of a role runs in a workload queue (D1050's `CREATE WORKLOAD QUEUE ... (CONCURRENCY n)`), which gives each role or tenant its own limit on statements running at once: the concurrency limit the quotas item left.
- **How.** Settings live in their own key space (0x23), keyed by role, database and lower-case name (empty names stand for "all"), and are applied when `identify_role` makes the session the role's.
- **Left.** `SET ... FROM CURRENT`, `ALTER DATABASE d SET`, the `pg_db_role_setting` catalog, and keeping a role from leaving its queue with its own `SET`.
- **Proof.** A roles test sets a role's `search_path`, an every-role and a role-and-database `application_name`, sees the most specific win at login, resets one and all and sees the every-role value come back, refuses an unknown role, and leaves nothing behind a dropped role.
