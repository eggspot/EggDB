## D414 MySQL's `WEEK()`, `YEARWEEK()`, `WEEKOFYEAR()` and `DATE_FORMAT()` — Decided by the code-only session (2026-11-10, D355); roadmap M10

- `WEEK(date[, mode])` follows MySQL's documented table of eight modes (first day Sunday or
  Monday, range 0–53 or 1–53, week 1 being the first week with a Sunday/Monday or with four or
  more days in the year). A mode is turned into three flags (Monday first, week year, first
  weekday) and the week is counted from the year's first day, moving a date into the previous
  year's last week, or the next year's week 1, as the flags say. Without a mode, `WEEK` and
  `YEARWEEK` use the session's new `default_week_format` system variable (0, clamped to 0–7).
- `YEARWEEK(date[, mode])` is `year * 100 + week` with the week-year flag always on;
  `WEEKOFYEAR(date)` is `WEEK(date, 3)`.
- `DATE_FORMAT(date, format)` spells every `%` specifier MySQL documents (`%a %b %c %D %d %e %f %H
  %h %I %i %j %k %l %M %m %p %r %S %s %T %U %u %V %v %W %w %X %x %Y %y %%`; any other `%c` gives `c`).
  A zero date formats its zero fields (`0000-00-00`) and gives `NULL` for the specifiers that need
  a real day (weekday names and numbers, day of year, weeks and week years, `%D`), and a `NULL`
  format gives `NULL`. Dates with a zero month or day other than the zero date are read as no
  date, so they give `NULL` where MySQL formats them: a known difference.
- The functions are internal names (`mysql_week`, `mysql_yearweek`, `mysql_date_format`) that
  only the MySQL dialect's parser produces, typed `BIGINT` and text.
